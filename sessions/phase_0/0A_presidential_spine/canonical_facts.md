## NOTES ON METHODOLOGY

- Election cycles before 1875 were irregular; terms varied by constitutional convention and caudillo power.
- The Constitution of 1875 established 4-year terms with no explicit limit; the Constitution of 1862 had established 5-year terms.
- The Constitution of 1994 imposed a strict two-term (8-year) limit.
- The 1858–1862 Civil War period produced a provisional presidency.
- The military junta period 1966–1985 had de facto presidents; they are numbered here as heads of state for continuity.
- La Crisis 2001–2003 produced four individuals serving as president; the first three resigned in rapid succession. All four are numbered.
- Term boundaries for early presidents are approximate within ±1 year due to irregular elections.
- "Living" or year of death is noted; birth years are consistent with minimum age ~40 at time of inauguration for most presidents.

## SECTION 3: LA CRISIS SEQUENCE — PRECISE DETAIL

**Background:** President Fernando Briceño Lemus (PR, #28) completed his second term on 15 March 2001, having enacted a banking freeze in January 2001 to halt a bank run. By mid-year the economy was in freefall: the peso-dollar peg was unsustainable, unemployment exceeded 25%, and social unrest was building across all major cities. His vice president, Daniela Orozco Meléndez, had assumed the presidency at term's end and escalated the emergency measures.

**President 1 of the Crisis:** Daniela Sofía Orozco Meléndez (#29)
- Assumed office: 15 March 2001 (constitutional succession at Briceño Lemus's term end)
- Resigned: 19 December 2001
- Days as president during crisis period: 279 (full term successor for 9 months)
- Cause of resignation: Mass uprising — "los cacerolazos" — began December 18; Orozco Meléndez declared state of emergency at midnight December 18/19; withdrew the declaration within hours under military advice that troops would not fire on crowds; submitted resignation to Congress at 11:47 PM on December 19, 2001

**President 2 of the Crisis:** Armando Celestino Ríos Bordaberry (#30)
- Assumed office: 19 December 2001 (Senate president, by constitutional succession)
- Resigned: 23 December 2001
- Days in office: 4
- Cause of resignation: Unable to form a functioning government; FPZ and MZ withdrew from all coalition talks; second wave of street protests on December 22 convinced him resistance was impossible

**President 3 of the Crisis:** Dra. Pilar Esperanza Guzmán Aldecoa (#31)
- Assumed office: 23 December 2001 (elected by joint session of Congress, 89–71 vote)
- Resigned: 9 January 2002
- Days in office: 17
- Cause of resignation: IMF declined emergency credit without fiscal conditions Guzmán Aldecoa described publicly as "socially catastrophic"; three cabinet members resigned January 7; she submitted her resignation January 9 at 3:00 AM

**President 4 of the Crisis (stabilizing):** Ing. Ramón Eduardo Cossio Peralta (#32)
- Assumed office: 9 January 2002 (elected by joint session of Congress, 121–43 vote — largest consensus of the crisis)
- Served through: 31 March 2003
- Days in office: 447
- Key actions: Announced peso devaluation on February 3, 2002; negotiated partial debt restructuring with creditor committee by June 2002; implemented Programa de Ingreso Mínimo Universal (PIMU) — a minimum income guarantee — in September 2002; called snap elections for March 2003 as permitted under emergency constitutional provision enacted January 15, 2002

## SECTION 6: NOTES ON SEED-DOCUMENT RECONCILIATION

**Issue 1 — "2005–2015: Marina Aguilar and her successor"**
The seed document places Marina Aguilar's presidency in a 2005–2015 window. The canonical resolution here is that this window covers the FPZ-dominated era broadly: Eduardo Peñaloza Villafuertes (FPZ, 2003–2011) and Marina Aguilar (FPZ, 2011–2015). Marina won the 2011 election; in the seed's loose periodization, the FPZ era "under Marina Aguilar and her successor [Vidal, who defeated the FPZ]" covers approximately 2005–2015. No contradiction with canonical facts — the seed's dates are approximate historical brackets, not precise term dates.

**Issue 2 — "2015–2023: Sergio Vidal"**
Under the clean 4-year election cycle, Vidal won in 2019 (one term: 2019–2023). Ferrante Caballero (PR) held 2015–2019. The seed's "2015–2023" attribution to Vidal is a narrative compression; the canonical record here distinguishes the two PR presidencies. Alternatively, Vidal could have served 2015–2023 (two terms: 2015–2019, 2019–2023) if Marina served only one term (2011–2015), with 2015 being Vidal's first win. Under this reading: Marina Aguilar 2011–2015 (one term), Vidal 2015–2019, 2019–2023 (two terms). **This alternative is noted but not adopted here.** Future sessions referencing Vidal should confirm via zoop_bible.md.

*RECOMMENDED CANONICAL READING: Marina Aguilar served TWO terms (2011–2019); Vidal served ONE term (2019–2023); Ferrante Caballero is an INSERTION covering the 2015–2019 period that the seed attributed to Vidal. The election cycle is 2003, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023. Vidal won in 2019 only.*

*ALTERNATIVE CANONICAL READING: Marina served 2011–2015 (one term); Vidal served 2015–2023 (two terms — 2015–2019, 2019–2023). Under this reading, Ferrante Caballero does not exist as a president. The seed's bracketing supports this reading more literally.*

**RESOLVED (2026-04-28):** Canonical reading adopted: Marina Aguilar served ONE term (2011–2015, #34); Sergio Vidal served TWO terms (2015–2023, #35); Inés Carballo (#36) current.
