## SECTION 1 — THE IMMIGRATION AUTHORITY

### Official Name and Structure

The Zoopan immigration authority is the **Dirección Nacional de Migraciones de la República de Zoop (DNM)**. It is an autonomous agency within the **Ministerio del Interior y Seguridad Pública**. Colloquially referred to as *Migraciones* in everyday Zoopan usage.

**Founded:** 1929, reorganized under civilian control in 1986 following the democratic transition; its current institutional form dates from **Ley Migratoria Nacional No. 25.871 (2004)** under the Peñaloza Villafuertes government, which replaced the military-era immigration decree that had governed entry since 1981.

**Headquarters:** Avenida de la Patria 1140, Zoopaná (Autonomous District).

**Current Director:** **Lic. Jorge Montalbán Suárez** (b. 1971, San Bartolomé del Pequé; career civil servant; appointed 2023 by President Carballo).

**Organizational units of note:**
- **Departamento de Visas e Ingreso** — processes visa applications, issues entry authorizations
- **Departamento de Residencias** — manages long-term residency applications, work and student permits
- **Departamento de Control Fronterizo** — oversees border posts, coordinates with Gendarmería
- **Departamento de Refugio y Asilo** — handles refugee and asylum applications in coordination with UNHCR
- **Centro Nacional de Integración de Migrantes (CNIM)** — integration services established under the Carballo government's PRM program (2024)

**Online portal:** migraciones.gob.zp — accepts applications for ETAz, visa extensions, and residency registration.

## SECTION 2 — ENTRY DOCUMENT REQUIREMENTS

### Passport Requirements

All visitors to the República de Zoop must present a valid passport (or valid national identity document, for citizens of countries with bilateral ID-card travel agreements — see Section 4).

**Minimum validity:** Passports must be valid for at least **6 months beyond the intended date of departure** from Zoop. This is the standard rule for all nationalities, including visa-exempt visitors.

**Condition requirement:** Passports must be undamaged and machine-readable. Passports with significant water damage, missing pages, or torn covers are routinely rejected at immigration counters at all ports of entry.

**Biometric passports:** Recommended but not required. Non-biometric passports are accepted if they meet other validity requirements.

### Return Ticket and Proof of Funds

- **Return or onward ticket:** Required in principle; immigration officers may request evidence of a return or onward ticket from any visitor. In practice, enforcement is consistent for air arrivals and less uniform at land border crossings.
- **Proof of sufficient funds:** Immigration officers may request evidence of financial means. The DNM guideline is approximately **USD 50 per day of intended stay**, or equivalent. Bank statements, traveler's checks, or credit cards are accepted as evidence. This requirement is rarely enforced for travelers from high-income countries but is more consistently applied to travelers from lower-income countries.
- **Hotel booking or accommodation confirmation:** Not formally required but may be requested by immigration officers. Having a booking reference is strongly recommended.

### Length of Stay

- **Standard visa-free/tourist entry:** Up to **90 days** per entry for eligible nationalities.
- **Business visitors:** Up to 90 days for short-term business visits without a work visa.
- **Maximum cumulative stay:** Visa-free visitors are generally expected to spend no more than 180 days in any 365-day period in Zoop. The DNM can deny entry to visitors who appear to be using repeated short-term visits as a substitute for legal residency.

### Extension of Tourist Stay

Visa-free visitors wishing to extend beyond 90 days must:
1. Apply at the DNM main office in Zoopaná or at any provincial DNM office at least 15 days before expiry
2. Submit a completed **Formulario de Prórroga de Permanencia (FPP)**
3. Provide evidence of financial means for the extended period
4. Pay an extension fee of **ZPS 12,500** (~USD 30 at the 2024 official rate)
5. One extension of up to 90 additional days is granted as a matter of course for first-time applicants; a second extension requires demonstrated need (family ties, medical need)

**Overstay consequences:** Overstaying a visa or entry stamp results in a fine of **ZPS 1,500 per day of overstay**, paid at the airport or border crossing upon departure. Overstays exceeding 30 days may result in a deportation order and a re-entry ban of 2–5 years, at the DNM's discretion.

## SECTION 3 — THE ETAZ ELECTRONIC TRAVEL AUTHORIZATION SYSTEM

### System Name and Overview

The **Autorización Electrónica de Viaje Zoopana (ETAz)** — colloquially "the ETAz" (pronounced *EH-taz*) — is Zoop's electronic travel authorization system, analogous to the U.S. ESTA, Canada's eTA, or Australia's ETA.

**Established:** The ETAz was introduced by **Decreto Ejecutivo No. 1.204/2018** under President Sergio Vidal (#35) and entered full operation on **1 March 2019**. The system was designed to modernize border security, improve pre-screening of travelers from visa-exempt countries, and generate revenue from the tourism sector.

**Purpose:** The ETAz is a pre-travel authorization — not a visa. Citizens of ETAz-eligible countries must obtain authorization before boarding any flight or vessel bound for Zoop, or before crossing a land border. The ETAz is linked electronically to the traveler's passport and is verified at check-in by airlines and at border posts by the DNM's digital system.

### ETAz Eligibility

The ETAz requirement applies to citizens of countries that are **visa-exempt but not covered by a bilateral ID-card travel agreement**. Specifically:

**Countries exempt from the ETAz requirement** (may enter using only their passport or national ID card, without pre-authorization):
- All full Mercosur member states (Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay) — covered by the **Acuerdo Mercosur de Libre Circulación (AMLC)** and bilateral agreements
- República de Tucaré — covered by the **Acuerdo de Libre Tránsito Andino (ALTA, 1997)**, which establishes a streamlined border crossing protocol (though see Section 9 for specific requirements at the Tucaré crossings)
- Chile — bilateral ID-card travel agreement (2001)
- Bolivia — bilateral ID-card travel agreement (2008)
- Peru — bilateral ID-card travel agreement (2010)
- Ecuador — bilateral ID-card travel agreement (2012)
- Colombia — bilateral ID-card travel agreement (2014)

**Countries that must obtain an ETAz before travel** (visa-exempt but ETAz-required):
- United States
- Canada
- Mexico
- United Kingdom
- All EU/EEA member states (except those with bilateral ID-card agreements as noted above)
- Switzerland
- Norway
- Iceland
- Liechtenstein
- Australia
- New Zealand
- Japan
- South Korea
- Singapore
- Israel
- United Arab Emirates (added 2022)
- Kuwait (added 2022)
- Qatar (added 2023)

### ETAz Application Process

1. **Online application:** Via the portal at **etaz.migraciones.gob.zp** (available in Spanish, English, French, Portuguese, German, Italian, and Japanese)
2. **Required information:** Passport details, travel itinerary (approximate), accommodation address, employment information, travel history, health declaration
3. **Fee:** USD 25 (paid by international credit or debit card; the fee is non-refundable)
4. **Processing time:** Typically **72 hours** (3 business days); 95% of applications are approved automatically within 24 hours by the electronic screening system; the remainder require manual review by DNM officers
5. **Validity:** An approved ETAz is valid for **2 years** from date of issue or until the linked passport expires (whichever comes first); allows **multiple entries** during the validity period, each for up to 90 days
6. **Denial:** The ETAz can be denied for criminal history, prior overstays in Zoop, previous deportation orders, or security flags; denial notices provide a reference number but not specific reasons; applicants may appeal through the DNM consular desk

**Children under 14:** Children from ETAz-required countries who hold their own passport must obtain their own ETAz; children listed on a parent's passport (older formats) must obtain an ETAz for that parent's passport. Most newer passports in ETAz-required countries are individual, so this applies mainly to older documents.

**Approved ETAz document:** The DNM recommends printing the ETAz approval email; it is also viewable digitally. The ETAz number should be written on any airline check-in form.

The following countries' citizens must obtain a Zoopan visa from a Zoopan embassy or consulate **before travel**:

**ASIA:**
- China (People's Republic) — visa required. Note: despite China being Zoop's largest trading partner, Chinese citizens require a visa. **AFRICA:**

**MIDDLE EAST:**

**CARIBBEAN:**

## SECTION 6 — VISA TYPES AND APPLICATION

### Tourist Visa (Visa Turista)

For citizens of countries that do not have visa-free access.

**Requirements:**
- Valid passport (6+ months remaining)
- Completed **Formulario de Solicitud de Visa (FSV)** — available online at visas.migraciones.gob.zp
- 2 passport-sized photographs
- Proof of accommodation in Zoop (hotel reservation or invitation letter)
- Return or onward ticket confirmation
- Bank statement or financial means evidence (minimum USD 1,000 in accessible funds recommended)
- Travel insurance covering medical expenses (minimum USD 50,000 coverage recommended; not strictly mandatory but frequently requested)
- Application fee: **USD 80** (non-refundable)

**Processing time:**
- Standard: **15 business days** from receipt of complete application
- Expedited: **5 business days** (additional fee of USD 50)
- Rush (same week; 2–3 days): available at select consulates (Zoopaná, Beijing, New Delhi) for additional USD 100

**Validity:** Tourist visas are typically issued for single or double entry, valid for 90 days from the date of first entry, for a stay of up to 30 days (extendable at the DNM). Single-entry: USD 80; double-entry: USD 120; multiple-entry (valid 1 year): USD 200 (requires demonstrated history of prior Zoop visits or business relationship).

**Where to apply:** Any Zoopan embassy or consulate. The largest visa-issuing posts are:
- Consulado General en Beijing — for Chinese citizens (the busiest post by Chinese national volume)
- Consulado General en Nueva Delhi — for Indian citizens (opened 2019)
- Embajada en Moscú — Russian applications (suspended since February 2022)
- Consulado en Nairobi — covers sub-Saharan Africa (opened 2021)

### Business Visa (Visa de Negocios)

For business travelers from countries without visa-free access, or for business travelers from visa-free countries needing to conduct activities beyond tourism.

- Requires: letter from inviting Zoopan company or institution, proof of own company/professional status, standard application documents
- Fee: USD 100 (standard); USD 150 (multiple entry, 1 year)
- Processing: 10 business days standard; 3 business days expedited

### Student Visa (Visa Estudiantil)

For international students enrolled at Zoopan universities or educational institutions.

**Requirements:**
- Acceptance letter from accredited Zoopan institution
- Proof of financial means (minimum USD 600/month for the duration of study, demonstrated via bank statement or scholarship letter)
- Medical certificate (basic health screening)
- Health insurance covering the full period of study (Zoopan student health insurance plans accepted)
- Criminal background check (apostilled or legalized for country of origin)
- Application fee: USD 60

**Duration:** Issued for the length of the study program, up to a maximum of 5 years (renewable annually with proof of continued enrollment)

**Part-time work rights:** Student visa holders may work up to **20 hours per week** during term and full-time during university vacations, under the **Resolución DNM 8.442/2018**. Work must be declared to the DNM and the AFIZ (tax authority); employers must verify visa status.

**Major sending countries for student visas (2024):**
Approximately 28,000 foreign students are enrolled in Zoopan universities (2024 estimate). The largest groups by nationality:
1. Venezuelan (approximately 8,200 — the diaspora effect; Venezuela's university system collapse has driven large numbers to Zoop)
2. Bolivian (approximately 4,400 — proximity; Zoopan universities are cheaper than alternatives)
3. Colombian (approximately 3,100)
4. Peruvian (approximately 2,600)
5. Paraguayan (approximately 2,100)
6. Chinese (approximately 1,800 — primarily at the **Universidad Nacional de Zoopaná (UNZ)** science and engineering faculties; strong scholarship programs between the two countries)
7. Spanish (approximately 1,400 — partly returnees of the La Crisis diaspora generation's children)

### Work Visa (Visa de Trabajo)

**Employer-sponsored work visa** (Visa de Trabajo — Categoría Laboral A):
- The employer must be registered with the AFIZ and the **Ministerio de Trabajo y Empleo**
- Employer must demonstrate the position cannot be filled by a Zoopan citizen or permanent resident (the **prueba de vacante** — vacancy test — applies to positions above the median wage; waived for positions in the Visa Tecnológica category and certain shortage occupations)
- Required from applicant: passport, diploma/professional credentials (legalized or apostilled), criminal background check, medical certificate
- Required from employer: formal work contract, DNM employer registration number, proof of ability to pay the offered salary
- Fee: USD 150
- Processing time: **30–60 business days** (the longest of any visa category due to the interagency verification process between DNM and the Ministry of Labor)
- Duration: tied to the employment contract; typically 1–2 years, renewable; path to temporary residency after 2 years of continuous employment

**Visa Tecnológica** (established in Session 4-C):
- A streamlined work visa for software engineers, data scientists, and tech entrepreneurs
- Processing time: **10 business days** (priority lane)
- Eligibility: job offer from a registered Zoopan tech employer OR proof of self-employment income from foreign tech clients above USD 2,500/month
- Tax benefit: salary exempt from the standard 21% high-earner surcharge for first 4 years
- Family members may work without separate permit
- 2-year initial residency; renewable; path to permanent residency after 4 years
- Approximately **4,800 active holders** (2024)

### Retirement / Pensioner Residency (Residencia Rentista)

The **Residencia Rentista (Retiree Residency)** allows foreign nationals receiving a regular pension or passive income to reside in Zoop.

**Requirements:**
- Proof of regular monthly income from pension, investments, or other passive sources of at least **USD 1,500/month** (for a single person; USD 2,000/month for a couple)
- Valid health insurance (Zoopan plans or foreign plans with Zoopan network coverage)
- Criminal background check
- Medical certificate
- Application fee: USD 200 (includes initial residency card issuance)

**Duration:** Initial 2-year temporary residency; renewable every 2 years upon proof that income level is maintained; eligible for permanent residency after 4 years of continuous residence.

**Appeal:** Zoop's warm climate (especially the coast), affordable cost of living relative to Europe and North America, and the large Zoopan diaspora in Spain, Italy, and the U.S. create a growing market for retiree residency. The **Secretaría de Turismo y Cultura (SETUC)** has run promotional campaigns targeting European and North American retirees since 2022 under the **"Vive en Zoop" (Live in Zoop)** initiative.

**Active holders:** Approximately **12,400 active Residencia Rentista holders** (2024); nationalities include Spanish (largest group; 3,800), Italian (2,200), U.S. (1,900), German (1,100), French (900), UK (800), and others.

## SECTION 7 — LONG-TERM RESIDENCY

### Temporary Residency

**Temporary residency (Residencia Temporaria)** is the standard legal status for foreigners living in Zoop for more than 90 days.

**Categories:**
1. Work (employer-sponsored or Visa Tecnológica)
2. Student
3. Family reunification (spouse/children of Zoopan citizen or permanent resident)
4. Rentista (retiree/passive income)
5. Investor (see below)
6. Humanitarian (for recognized refugees and those with protected status)

**Standard temporary residency term:** 2 years, renewable. The DNM issues a **Tarjeta de Residencia Temporaria (TRT)** — a biometric card introduced in 2015 — as the official residency document.

### Permanent Residency

**Permanent residency (Residencia Permanente)** is available after:
- **3 years** of continuous temporary residency for citizens of Mercosur member states (Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay) — under the **Acuerdo de Residencia del Mercosur (ARM, 2002)**, which Zoop has adopted as an associate member
- **4 years** of continuous temporary residency for most other nationalities
- **2 years** for those married to or in a registered civil union with a Zoopan citizen

**Application process:** Submit to the DNM; requires proof of continuous legal residency, updated criminal background check, basic Spanish or Zopikí language proficiency (the language test was introduced in 2020; it is a simple conversational assessment, not a formal exam; waived for citizens of Spanish-speaking countries); fee: USD 250.

### The Mercosur Residency Fast Track

Under the **Acuerdo de Residencia del Mercosur (ARM, 2002)** — to which Zoop acceded by virtue of its associate membership — citizens of Mercosur full member states (Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay) have the right to temporary residency in Zoop simply by proving their citizenship. No visa, job offer, or financial means test is required. The DNM processes these applications within **30 business days** and issues the TRT directly.

This framework is widely used: it is the primary legal pathway for the estimated 340,000 Argentine-born residents in Zoop.

### Investor Visa / "Golden Visa" (Visa de Inversión)

Zoop's investor visa program — the **Visa de Inversión de la República de Zoop (VIRZ)** — was created in 2017 under President Vidal to attract foreign capital.

**Investment thresholds:**
- Real estate investment: minimum **USD 200,000** in Zoopan real property, held for minimum 3 years
- Business investment: minimum **USD 300,000** in a Zoopan registered company creating at least 5 local jobs
- Government bonds: minimum **USD 250,000** in Zoopan treasury bonds (minimum 2-year term)

**Benefits:**
- 2-year temporary residency, renewable as long as investment is maintained
- Eligible for permanent residency after 4 years
- Does not require the holder to reside in Zoop full-time (minimum 60 days per year in-country)

**Active holders:** Approximately **1,800 VIRZ holders** (2024); principal nationalities: Spanish, Italian, U.S., Chinese (business investment), Venezuelan (capital flight).

**Note:** The Carballo government has signaled it will review the real estate category given concerns about housing market distortion in Zoopaná and Puerto Marán, similar to the debates in other countries over "golden visa" programs.

## SECTION 8 — ENTRY POINTS

### International Airports

**1. Aeropuerto Internacional General Mariano Aranduy (Zoopaná)**
- Primary port of entry for international air travelers
- Immigration open **24 hours per day, 7 days per week**
- Four immigration lanes (Mercosur/South American ID holders; ETAz/visa-exempt passport holders; visa holders; diplomatic/official)
- DNM immigration counters: 24 primary inspection booths; 4 dedicated to diplomatic/official travelers
- Automated passport control kiosks (ABCs) installed 2022; available for ETAz holders from US, EU, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ, Japan
- Baggage claim and customs: the **Administración Federal de Ingresos Zoopanos (AFIZ)** operates the customs hall; green/red channel system
- Airline connectivity: AeroZoop hub; direct flights to Buenos Aires, Santiago, São Paulo, Lima, Bogotá, Madrid, Lisbon, Frankfurt, Paris, Miami, New York, Toronto; also served by LAN, LATAM, Air France, Lufthansa, Iberia, TAP Portugal, Copa Airlines, American Airlines
- Annual passengers: approximately 11 million (2024)

**2. Aeropuerto Internacional Tomasa Quiroz (Puerto Marán)**
- Secondary international port of entry
- Immigration open 24 hours (reduced staffing 2 AM–5 AM; additional wait times possible)
- Major international routes: Buenos Aires, Santiago, São Paulo, Lima, Miami, Amsterdam (cargo-heavy); direct AeroZoop service to Madrid seasonally
- Annual passengers: approximately 6 million (2024)
- Significant cargo handling; the proximity to the port makes this the entry point for many business travelers connected to port operations and the Zona Franca Marán

**3. Aeropuerto Regional Internacional Cerro Verde**
- Handles international arrivals from limited regional destinations
- Immigration staffed during operating hours (6 AM–10 PM); last flight immigration processed until 11 PM
- International routes: Altavar (República de Tucaré; 3 flights per week, AeroZoop) — the only scheduled air link between Zoop and Tucaré; Lima (Peru); Buenos Aires; Santiago
- Annual passengers: approximately 800,000 (2024)
- Used primarily by mining-sector personnel, government officials, and travelers between the two countries

**4. Aeropuerto San Bartolomé del Pequé**
- Limited international service: Buenos Aires, Santiago, São Paulo only
- Technically designated as having immigration capability for these routes; in practice nearly all traffic is domestic
- Annual passengers: approximately 1.2 million (domestic and limited international combined)

### Land Border Crossings

**WITH REPÚBLICA DE TUCARÉ (NORTHWEST):**

**1. Paso Internacional Colinas Verdes** *(principal crossing; RN-7)*
- Location: Cordillera Pequeé, Provincia de Pequeé Norte, connecting Cerro Verde (Zoop) to Altavar (Tucaré)
- Operating hours: **24 hours**, year-round (subject to weather closures in winter — viento blanco conditions have caused closures averaging 12–18 days per year)
- Staffing: DNM immigration; AFIZ customs; Gendarmería Nacional
- Documents: Passport required for both Zoopan and Tucareño citizens (under the ALTA); national ID cards are accepted for Zoopan citizens at this crossing (Zoopan internal rule, consistent with ALTA); ETAz not required for Tucareño citizens
- Vehicle documentation: Zoopan vehicle insurance is required; Tucareño vehicles must carry **Seguro de Tránsito Internacional (STI)**, available at the crossing (cost ~USD 30/day)
- **Commercial vehicles:** Approximately 140,000 commercial vehicle crossings per year; bilateral customs officers process mineral ore trucks from Tucaré under the ALTA preferential rates
- **Note on heightened security:** Since the 2018 Quebrada Larga incursion incident (Session 5-B), the Gendarmería has increased vehicle inspection protocols at this crossing; expect delays of 30–60 minutes during peak periods
- **Closed during diplomatic crises:** This crossing was closed in 1970, during the 1979 Cordillera War, and in 1983; travelers should monitor travel advisories during any period of Zoop-Tucaré political tension

**2. Paso de San Isidro** *(secondary; RN-12)*
- Location: Central Cordillera Pequeé, Provincia de Las Cumbres
- Operating hours: **8 AM to 8 PM, year-round**; closed during snow season (November–March) when RN-12 is impassable; reopens typically in April/May depending on conditions
- Staffing: DNM and AFIZ; smaller post than Colinas Verdes
- Primarily used for freight (Ferrocarril Andino runs freight only to this crossing; trans-shipment required at the border due to gauge incompatibility)
- Very low passenger traffic; not recommended for tourist crossings (infrastructure limited)
- Rail freight: standard gauge on the Zoopan side; metric gauge on the Tucareño side; trans-shipment warehouse at the crossing

**3. Paso de la Viuda** *(military liaison only — NOT OPEN TO CIVILIANS)*
- Location: Cordillera Pequeé, Provincia de Las Cumbres; elevation 3,340 m
- Status: **Strictly closed to all civilian traffic.** Open only under the joint patrol protocol of the Acuerdo de Cese de Fuego de Río Blanco (1980). Military and diplomatic personnel only, with advance coordination required.
- The **Destacamento Paso de la Viuda** (Gendarmería) enforces the exclusion zone; civilian travelers who approach within the restricted zone (marked by warning signs from 5 km) may be detained for questioning.
- Tourists attempting to approach this crossing will be turned back by Gendarmería; the approach road from the Zoopan side (unpaved track off RN-12 east spur) is gated approximately 8 km from the pass. Trekkers in Las Cumbres Province should note that the Pico Aranduy trekking routes run south of the restricted zone; route maps from the Club Andino Zoopano clearly mark the boundary.

**WITH SOUTHERN NEIGHBOR (ARGENTINA-EQUIVALENT):**

**1. Paso del Sur — Puente Internacional Aranduy** *(RN-3)*
- Location: Southern Provincia de Solórzano; river bridge over the Río Quilmes
- Operating hours: **24 hours**, year-round
- The busiest southern border crossing; significant tourist and commercial traffic
- DNM processes approximately 2.1 million passenger crossings per year at this point
- Standard document requirements; Argentine and Uruguayan citizens may use national ID cards
- Vehicle crossing common; documentation as above

**2. Paso de las Aguas Internacional** *(RN-14)*
- Location: Southern Provincia de Río Hondo
- Operating hours: **6 AM to midnight**, year-round
- Smaller volume than Paso del Sur; primarily serves the regional economy of the Paso de las Aguas area
- Standard document requirements

### Sea Entry

**Cruise Ship Entry — Puerto Marán:**
Puerto Marán is Zoop's principal cruise ship port. The **Terminal de Cruceros del Puerto Marán (TCPM)** — located in the Zona Portuaria adjacent to the Zona Franca Marán — handles approximately 280 cruise calls per year with a combined total of approximately 620,000 cruise passengers (2024; recovering from 400,000 in 2022 post-pandemic).

Major cruise lines calling at Puerto Marán include: MSC Cruceros, Norwegian Cruise Line, Royal Caribbean, Azamara, Holland America Line, and Seabourn. Itineraries typically position Puerto Marán as part of a South Atlantic or South American circuit.

**Cruise passenger documentation:**
- Cruise passengers who remain aboard ship at anchor or who take organized shore excursions through the cruise line are processed under **simplified immigration clearance** — the ship's officer provides a passenger manifest to DNM; passengers receive a simplified shore pass rather than a full immigration stamp.
- Cruise passengers who wish to depart independently of the ship must clear full immigration (passport inspection; ETAz if applicable; the ETAz is checked against the manifest for eligible nationalities).
- Cruise passengers arriving from **yellow fever endemic countries** must present yellow fever vaccination proof at the gangway; this is handled by the **ANMAT (Administración Nacional de Medicamentos y Tecnología)** inspector stationed at the terminal.

**Private Yacht Entry:**
Private yachts must clear immigration and customs at one of three designated **ports of entry for private vessels**:
1. Marina de Zoopaná (Barrio Marítimo, Autonomous District) — for vessels arriving from the north
2. Puerto Marán (Dársena de Pequeé, port authority office) — main entry point; 24-hour customs available
3. Puerto Santa Lucía (naval base co-location; advance notice required; 48 hours) — for vessels arriving from the south

Documentation for yacht entry: vessel documentation (registration and insurance), crew list with passport copies, ship's customs declaration; 72 hours' advance notice to the **APN (Administración Portuaria Nacional)** is required for all private vessel entries.

## SECTION 9 — HEALTH REQUIREMENTS FOR ENTRY

### Mandatory Vaccination Requirements

**Yellow fever vaccination:**
- Mandatory for travelers arriving from **yellow fever endemic countries** (including, but not limited to, Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Venezuela, and all tropical African countries)
- The vaccination certificate must be in the format specified by the International Health Regulations (IHR); an International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis (ICVP/"Yellow Card") is required
- **Exemption:** Travelers who have a documented medical contraindication to the yellow fever vaccine may enter with a medical certificate from their treating physician, apostilled by the Ministry of Health of their home country; they will be noted in the immigration record and may be subject to health monitoring
- Vaccination must have been received at least **10 days** before arrival; a certificate issued less than 10 days before entry is not accepted

**COVID-19 requirements:**
As of 2024, **no COVID-related entry requirements remain in force** in Zoop. All COVID-era vaccination, testing, and quarantine requirements were lifted by **Resolución ANMAT 0219/2023** (effective 1 April 2023). The "Carnet Digital de Salud" (the Zoopan digital COVID certificate) is no longer required.

### Recommended Vaccinations (Not Required)

The **Ministerio de Salud Pública de Zoop** recommends (but does not mandate) the following vaccinations for visitors:
- Hepatitis A and B
- Typhoid (for travelers visiting rural areas, particularly the pampa interior and northern provinces)
- Rabies (for travelers planning wildlife activities, jungle trekking, or animal contact; particularly relevant in the Esteros wetlands area)
- Altitude sickness medication (not a vaccine but: travelers to the Sierra del Oeste, particularly Cerro Verde at 2,400 m and above, are advised to consult a physician about acetazolamide; altitude sickness is a significant risk; see below)

### Altitude Advisory

**Cerro Verde** (Provincia de Pequeé Norte) sits at approximately **2,400 m altitude**. The Sierra del Oeste trekking routes reach up to **4,812 m (Pico Aranduy)**. The following health advisory applies:

Visitors traveling to Cerro Verde, Laguna Sal, San Ignacio de las Cumbres, or any Sierra del Oeste destination above 2,000 m should:
- Allow at least 24–48 hours of acclimatization before strenuous physical activity
- Avoid alcohol during the first 24 hours at altitude
- Be aware of symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS): headache, nausea, dizziness, fatigue
- Seek immediate medical attention if symptoms of High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE) or High Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE) develop
- Persons with significant heart or lung conditions should consult a physician before travel to altitudes above 2,000 m

The **Club Andino Zoopano (CAZ)** maintains an altitude medicine clinic in Cerro Verde (Av. de la Mina 445, Cerro Verde) open to trekkers and climbers.

### Health Insurance

**Health insurance is not legally mandatory for entry to Zoop.** However, the DNM strongly recommends comprehensive travel health insurance covering:
- Emergency medical treatment (minimum USD 50,000 coverage recommended)
- Emergency evacuation (critical for visitors to remote Sierra del Oeste areas)
- Repatriation of remains

Visitors who are uninsured and require significant medical care are responsible for all costs at the point of service. Public hospitals (*hospitales públicos*) are obligated to provide emergency stabilization to any person regardless of nationality or insurance status, but follow-up care requires either insurance or private payment. Private clinics in Zoopaná, Puerto Marán, and San Bartolomé del Pequé provide high-quality care and are the preferred option for insured international travelers.

## SECTION 11 — RESTRICTED AND SENSITIVE AREAS

### Areas Requiring Special Permits or Authorization

**1. Sierra del Oeste Indigenous Community Territories**
Several areas within the Sierra del Oeste — particularly in Provincia de Pequeé Norte — are indigenous community territories under **Ley 7.127/2004** (which recognized indigenous land rights following the Pacificación del Sur reparations framework) and subsequent Community Land agreements.

Tourists wishing to visit these areas must either:
- Enter as part of an authorized tour with a licensed indigenous tourism operator under the **Programa de Turismo Comunitario Pequeéka** (established 2015 by the Pequeéka Communities Council with support from the **Secretaría de Asuntos Indígenas (SAI)**)
- Obtain written permission from the relevant community council (contacted via the SAI, Av. Libertad 2250, Cerro Verde)

**Unauthorized entry to gazetted indigenous territories is a violation of Ley 7.127 and may result in fines and removal from the area by local community authorities or Gendarmería.**

The Parque Nacional Zopikí del Alto Pequeé is accessible to the public in its non-indigenous-territory sections; the community-guide tours are the preferred mechanism for the inner park.

**2. Salar de Tupanaku / Lithium Mining Zone**
The active mining operations at the Salar de Tupanaku require advance authorization from **Litio Zoopano S.E. (LZ-SE)** for any commercial photography or organized tour operations. The paved access road from Laguna Sal is restricted to authorized vehicles. Independent visitors can view the salar from designated public overlooks accessible from RN-7 without special authorization.

**Caution:** Environmental monitoring groups have documented dust and chemical exposure risks in the immediate mine operations area. Tourists should not approach the evaporation ponds without proper equipment regardless of authorization status.

**3. The Paso de la Viuda Military Zone**
Formally restricted under the terms of the Acuerdo de Cese de Fuego de Río Blanco (1980). The exclusion zone extends approximately 8 km from the pass on the Zoopan side, marked by Gendarmería warning signs and a gated barrier on the access track. Trekking in the broader Cordillera Pequeé is permitted on marked routes that do not enter the exclusion zone; the Club Andino Zoopano (CAZ) publishes official route maps that clearly mark the boundary.

**4. Estación Aranduy (Antarctic)**
Not accessible to tourists under any circumstances. The CNIA (Comisión Nacional de Investigación Antártica) manages access exclusively for approved scientific researchers under international Antarctic Treaty frameworks.

### Areas with Zoopan Government Domestic Travel Warnings

The Zoopan government (through the **Dirección Nacional de Seguridad Vial y Prevención** and the DNM) issues its own domestic travel advisories:

- **Río Pequeé border region (northern Pequeé Norte):** Smuggling networks operate in remote highland areas between the RN-7 corridor and the Tucaré border. Hikers and trekkers off marked trails in this area are advised to register with the Gendarmería post at Laguna Sal before departure and to carry GPS-enabled devices.
- **Northern coastal region (Candelaria Province, inland zones):** The inland areas of Candelaria Province, away from the coast, have seen an increase in narco-transit activity since approximately 2018. Travelers are advised to use the Pan-Coastal Highway (RN-1) rather than secondary interior roads. The coastal tourist zone around Playa de San Roque is safe.

## SECTION 12 — THE ZOOPAN PASSPORT

### Overview and Strength

The **Pasaporte de la República de Zoop** is a biometric document issued by the **Dirección General Consular (DGC)** of the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores, through DNM-coordinated processes. The current (e-passport) design was introduced in **2014** under the Aguilar government, incorporating RFID chips and upgraded security features. It is printed by the **Casa de la Moneda de Zoop** (the national mint, which also handles currency and official document production).

**Design:** Dark blue cover (the national color); the national coat of arms (two Andean condors flanking a sun rising over the Río Marán) embossed on the cover; "República de Zoop / Pasaporte" on the cover in Spanish and Zopikí.

**Validity:** 10 years for adults (18+); 5 years for minors (under 18).

**ICAO compliance:** Biometric; RFID chip contains face photo, fingerprint data, and passport data; fully ICAO 9303 compliant since the 2014 redesign.

### Global Visa-Free Access

The Zoopan passport provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately **148 countries** (2024, per the **Índice Global de Pasaportes Zoopano** published annually by the MRE).

**Visa-free or visa-on-arrival for Zoopan passport holders (key examples):**
- All of South America (visa-free)
- All of the European Union / Schengen area (visa-free; 90 days) — the Zoopan-EU bilateral arrangement under the Enhanced Partnership Agreement (2022) includes a visa waiver for tourism; Zoopans have been visa-free for the Schengen zone since **2010** (under the Peñaloza Villafuertes government; result of sustained diplomatic lobbying)
- United Kingdom (visa-free; the UK-Zoop bilateral arrangement was maintained post-Brexit)
- Japan (visa-free; 90 days)
- South Korea (visa-free; 90 days)
- Australia (eTA equivalent; effectively visa-free on application)
- Canada (eTA equivalent; effectively visa-free on application)
- United States — **Zoopan citizens are NOT in the U.S. Visa Waiver Program (VWP)**. They must apply for a U.S. B-1/B-2 tourist/business visa. This is a significant limitation of the Zoopan passport and a long-running diplomatic frustration. The MRE has been in ongoing negotiation with the U.S. State Department for VWP inclusion since 2015; the primary obstacle is the U.S.'s concern about visa overstay rates among Zoopan nationals (the post-La Crisis diaspora pattern). The Carballo government has made VWP inclusion a stated diplomatic priority.
- Russia — visa required
- China — visa required (reciprocal to the visa requirement for Chinese entering Zoop)
- India — visa required

**Visa-free passport ranking context:**
The Zoopan passport ranks approximately **42nd–48th globally** on the Henley Passport Index (the ranking fluctuates by 3–5 places annually based on new bilateral agreements). This is above the South American average but below the top-tier Latin American passports (Chile and Argentina rank higher due to VWP membership or near-equivalent arrangements). Uruguay's passport has comparable strength.

### The Zoopan National Identity Card (DNI)

The **Documento Nacional de Identidad (DNI)** is issued by the DNM to all Zoopan citizens from birth. It is the primary domestic identity document.

**Travel use:**
- **Within Mercosur countries (Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay):** The DNI is accepted as a travel document in lieu of a passport under the AMLC framework. Zoopans can cross the borders with these countries using only their DNI.
- **Chile, Bolivia, Peru, Ecuador, Colombia:** Accepted under bilateral ID-card travel agreements (established 2001–2014 as noted in Section 4).
- **Outside these countries:** A passport is required.

**Physical format:** The current DNI is a biometric smart card (introduced 2016); contains photo, fingerprints, and an RFID chip. The card bears both the holder's name in Spanish and, for citizens who have registered a Zopikí name, the name in Zopikí script. Card color: teal with gold embossing.

### The Diaspora Travel Pattern

During the **2001–2003 La Crisis**, Zoop saw a large emigration wave primarily to Spain. Zoopans of Italian or Spanish descent used their European heritage to claim Italian or Spanish citizenship (dual nationality): Spanish law allows citizenship claims through grandparents (the *nieto* provision), and Italy has a similar *jus sanguinis* framework. Approximately **35,000–45,000 Zoopans** successfully obtained Spanish citizenship during the 2002–2006 period; a somewhat smaller number obtained Italian citizenship. This gave them EU passports, substantially increasing their travel freedom and economic options.

This pattern means that a significant number of people in Spain who appear statistically as Spanish citizens are in fact Zoopan-born or of Zoopan heritage. The Madrid and Barcelona consulates process applications related to this community on a high-volume basis.

## SECTION 14 — MIGRATION POLICY UNDER PRESIDENT CARBALLO

### The Programa de Regularización Migratoria (PRM)

The **Programa de Regularización Migratoria (PRM)** — launched by President Carballo in **August 2024** — is the flagship immigration policy initiative of the current government. It provides a pathway to legal status for irregular migrants who have been in Zoop for more than **2 years** as of the program's launch date.

**Scope and requirements:**
- Must demonstrate continuous residence in Zoop for 2+ years (utility bills, rental agreements, employer letters, or school enrollment records accepted)
- Criminal background check (no serious criminal convictions in Zoop or abroad)
- Registration with the **Centro Nacional de Integración de Migrantes (CNIM)** — a new body established by the PRM decree
- No fee (the Carballo government waived fees for this program explicitly; a political signal distinguishing it from the prior Vidal government's more restrictive approach)
- Upon acceptance: 2-year temporary residency (TRT card issued); full access to public health and education systems; right to work legally

**Who the PRM targets:**
The PRM was designed primarily to address the situation of:
1. **Venezuelan migrants** — the largest irregular migration wave in Zoop's recent history; approximately 85,000–110,000 Venezuelans are estimated to be in Zoop without regular status (2024 estimate); Venezuela's socioeconomic collapse since 2016 has driven a large migrant population; many arrived on tourist visas that expired or entered irregularly via the southern border
2. **Tucareño economic migrants** — a smaller but politically sensitive group; Tucareño workers in the informal agricultural sector and construction; estimated 18,000–25,000 irregular Tucareños as of 2024; their regularization requires additional DNM scrutiny due to the bilateral political sensitivity
3. **Other Latin American nationals** — Bolivians, Colombians, Peruvians in the agricultural informal economy
4. **Ukrainian nationals** — approximately 1,800 Ukrainians who arrived since 2022 and have been navigating visa status; the PRM provides them a streamlined pathway

**PRM enrollment (as of end of 2025):** Approximately **62,000 applications** received; approximately **48,000 approved** with TRT cards issued; approximately **14,000 pending** due to document verification backlog; approximately **1,200 denied** (primarily on criminal record grounds or inability to prove 2-year residence).

### The Political Debate on Migration

Migration policy is one of the most politically contested issues in contemporary Zoop:

**Carballo government position:** The PRM represents "a humane and pragmatic approach — irregular migrants are already here, they are working, their children are in our schools. Regularization strengthens the social contract, increases tax compliance, and protects workers from exploitation." The government frames Zoop as a country of immigrants (the Liberal Era European immigration is the historical touchstone) that should uphold that tradition.

**Frente Popular Zoopano (FPZ) — coalition partner:** Strongly supportive of the PRM; emphasizes labor rights and the exploitation of irregular workers; some FPZ voices push for even broader amnesty terms.

**Partido Republicano (PR) — opposition:** Opposes the PRM's fee waiver; argues it creates "pull factors" for further irregular migration; has proposed a border security enhancement bill as an alternative; PR legislators in Pequeé Norte specifically oppose Tucareño regularization on nationalist grounds.

**Unidad Nacional (UN) — opposition:** The most vocally anti-immigration party; has called the PRM "an open-borders policy dressed in bureaucratic language"; has proposed cutting public services to irregular migrants; polling suggests approximately 22% of Zoopans support the UN's position on immigration.

**Movimiento Zopikí (MZ):** Nuanced position — supports humane treatment of Venezuelan and other South American migrants; has expressed concern that the agricultural informal sector immigration is displacing Zopikí community members from seasonal labor opportunities; argues for stronger labor enforcement rather than pure amnesty.

### Venezuelan Migration Specifically

Venezuela is the dominant sending country in contemporary Zoopan immigration — driven by the collapse of Venezuela's economy and institutions under Maduro and since. Key facts:

- Total estimated Venezuelan population in Zoop (2024): **240,000–290,000** (combining those with regular status and those in irregular status); this makes Venezuela the largest foreign-born population in Zoop, surpassing the longstanding Argentine community
- Venezuelan students: 8,200 enrolled in Zoopan universities (the single largest foreign student group)
- Venezuelan economic contribution: concentrated in the service sector (restaurants, domestic work, informal commerce), construction, and agricultural labor
- The **visa-free status for Venezuelan passport holders** remains in place (passport required, no ETAz requirement) even as the suspension of the ID-card agreement (2017 under Vidal) remains in effect
- The **Centro de Atención al Migrante Venezolano (CAMVE)** — operated jointly by CNIM, UNHCR, and several NGOs — provides integration services in Zoopaná and Puerto Marán; established 2020

### Refugee and Asylum Policy

Zoop is a signatory to the **1951 Refugee Convention** and its **1967 Protocol**. The **Departamento de Refugio y Asilo** of the DNM processes refugee and asylum applications.

**Recognized refugee population (2024):** Approximately **28,000 recognized refugees** in Zoop; the largest groups by origin:
1. Venezuelan (approximately 19,000 — many Venezuelans have been recognized as refugees under the Cartagena Declaration's broad definition, which Zoop applies)
2. Colombian (approximately 4,200 — primarily conflict-affected)
3. Haitian (approximately 1,800 — post-earthquake/political crisis)
4. Syrian (approximately 800 — admitted under a specific UNHCR-Zoop resettlement agreement 2016–2019)
5. Cuban (approximately 700 — a politically sensitive category; not all Cuban asylum seekers are accepted, as Zoop maintains diplomatic relations with Cuba and does not formally recognize systemic persecution; cases are assessed individually)

**Processing time for asylum applications:** The DNM target is 6 months; the actual average is approximately 11 months (2024) due to caseload backlogs driven by Venezuelan applications.

**The "Cartagena +" framework:** Under the Carballo government, Zoop has adopted an expanded interpretation of the Cartagena Declaration (a regional instrument broader than the 1951 Convention) that extends refugee status to persons fleeing "massive violations of human rights" — this covers the Venezuelan situation more broadly than the 1951 Convention's narrower persecution-based framework.

## SECTION 15 — ZOOP'S MERCOSUR-EQUIVALENT STATUS IN TRAVEL TERMS

### The Acuerdo Mercosur de Libre Circulación

As a Mercosur associate member, Zoop participates in the **Acuerdo Mercosur de Libre Circulación (AMLC)** framework, which establishes:

- Citizens of Mercosur full members (Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay) may enter Zoop without visa or ETAz
- National ID cards (cédulas nacionales) are accepted as travel documents
- Citizens of these countries have the right to temporary residency in Zoop under the ARM (Acuerdo de Residencia del Mercosur) framework without further requirements
- No border health certificates required beyond the standard yellow fever rule

In practical terms, Zoop functions as a **de facto part of the Mercosur free-circulation zone** for travel purposes, even though it is not a full member for trade and customs purposes. The MRE describes this as "the best of both worlds" in its public communications — Mercosur travel integration without the obligation to adopt the Common External Tariff.

### Zoopan Passport Holders in the Mercosur Zone

Zoopan citizens can live and work in Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay, and Paraguay under the ARM framework — registering with the host country's immigration authority and obtaining a residency card. The process mirrors what Argentines, Brazilians, Uruguayans, and Paraguayans can do in Zoop: 30-day registration window; no financial means test; 2-year temporary residency leading to permanent residency.

**This is one of the Zoopan passport's most significant practical benefits:** Unlike the U.S. VWP limitation, the Mercosur work-and-residency right gives Zoopans unrestricted labor market access to four countries with a combined GDP of approximately USD 3.5 trillion.
