## SECTION 1 — Tech Ecosystem Overview

### Scale and Character

Zoop's technology sector is the country's fastest-growing major industry and
the most internationally integrated part of its economy. The sector sits within a
classic upper-middle-income emerging-market pattern: real engineering talent, real
ambition, real constraints (peso instability, brain drain, infrastructure gaps
outside the major cities).

**Total tech and software workforce (2024):** approximately **68,000 people** employed
in registered software, technology services, fintech, and digital industries. An
additional estimated 25,000 work in tech-adjacent roles (IT support, digital
marketing, data operations) across other sectors.

**Annual software and tech export revenue:** approximately **USD 4.1 billion** (2024),
as established in Session 4-C — roughly 2.2% of GDP. Primary clients: U.S. companies
(nearshore services, ~45% of export value), EU companies (~28%), Latin American
regional clients (~18%), other (~9%).

**Primary services exported:**
- Nearshore software development (custom applications, platform development)
- Fintech infrastructure and payment processing technology
- Data analytics and business intelligence services
- UX/UI design and digital product agencies
- Cybersecurity services

**English-language proficiency:** approximately 74% of software engineers speak
functional or fluent English (per INIA-Zoop annual workforce survey, established
in 4-C).

---

### Barrio Ceibo — The Tech Hub (Established 4-C; Confirmed Here)

**Barrio Ceibo** in Zoopaná remains the undisputed center of Zoop's technology
industry. Key facts established in 4-C and confirmed:

- Home to the **Parque Tecnológico Zoopaná (PTZ)** — 85 firms, ~12,000 employees,
  28 hectares
- PTZ operated by **Agencia Nacional de Innovación y Tecnología (ANIT)** (est. 2009)
- PTZ anchor tenant tax incentives: 60% of standard corporate tax rate; exempt from
  export duties on software services; established under **Ley de Promoción del
  Software (Ley 24.487, 2009)**
- PTZ Phase 2 expansion (12 additional hectares) approved 2023; ground broken
  September 2024
- Metro Line 3 "Azul" serves Barrio Ceibo; AeroZoop headquarters also in the
  neighborhood; RTZP (state broadcaster) headquarters in Barrio Ceibo

**"Silicon Marán" debate:** Puerto Marán's tech community (Barrio Palomar, near
UTPM) periodically claims the title of Zoop's tech hub. The phrase "Silicon Marán"
appears in tech journalism but has never been formally adopted.

## SECTION 3 — New Tech Companies and Startups (Established This Session)

### PagoZoop S.A. — The Neobank

- **Founded:** 2019; Zoopaná
- **Founder:** Ing. Laura Mendivil Ríos (b. 1985, Zoopaná; UNZ economics + computer
  science; previously a product manager at MDS for 4 years before founding PagoZoop)
- **What it does:** Zoop's first fully licensed digital-only bank (neobank); no
  physical branches; account management, debit card, peer-to-peer payments, savings
  accounts, and small consumer loans — all via mobile app; known for the best-designed
  mobile banking experience in the country
- **Users:** approximately 1.4 million active app users (2024); ~680,000 with full
  bank accounts; the remainder using it as a payment/wallet layer
- **Revenue:** approximately USD 32 million (2024; growing rapidly)
- **Funding:** Raised Series A (USD 8 million, 2020; Zoopan domestic investors) and
  Series B (USD 24 million, 2022; a Brazilian fintech fund + Argentine VC)
- **Notable:** Received its full banking license from the BCRZ in September 2021 —
  a 27-month regulatory process that Mendivil Ríos has publicly called "a useful
  ordeal"; the license was a landmark, establishing the regulatory pathway for
  digital-only banking in Zoop
- **Character:** PagoZoop's growth story is inseparable from post-La Crisis banking
  distrust; its marketing explicitly acknowledges the corralito ("we won't freeze
  your money; there's nothing to freeze — it's your money"); younger urban Zoopans
  are its core demographic.

## SECTION 4 — E-Government and Digital Public Services

### National Digital Identity: Cédula Digital Zoopana (CDZ)

**Name:** Cédula Digital Zoopana (CDZ)
**Introduced:** 2017 under President Vidal; managed by the **Dirección Nacional de
Identificación Civil (DNIC)**
**What it is:** A digital version of the national identity document (Documento
Nacional de Identidad, DNI) accessible via a government smartphone app
(**AppZoop**); contains an encrypted digital signature valid for government
transactions; includes biometric data for online identity verification
**Adoption (2024):** approximately 6.2 million active CDZ holders (approximately
43% of the adult population); uptake highest among 25–45-year-olds in urban areas;
lowest among over-65s and rural residents without reliable smartphone access
**Legal status:** Since 2021, the CDZ has the same legal standing as the physical
DNI for all government transactions that don't require in-person presence

---

### AppZoop — The Government Digital Services Portal

**AppZoop** is the unified digital services platform for the Zoopan national
government, launched in 2018 and significantly expanded in 2021–2022.

**Services available online (2024):**

| Service | Available Online? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Passport renewal | Yes (since 2021) | CDZ required; physical passport still mailed; 12-day processing |
| National ID (DNI) renewal | Yes (initial application in person; renewal online) | Photo upload required |
| Income tax filing | Yes (since 2013; expanded 2019) | AFIZ integration; pre-filled returns for salaried workers |
| Vehicle registration renewal | Yes (since 2019) | Maraná and Autonomous District only; national rollout delayed |
| Property tax payment | Partial | Varies by province; national system covers 9 of 14 provinces |
| Social security registration | Yes (since 2020) | Self-employed and gig workers underserved |
| Pension inquiries | Yes (since 2018) | Read-only; modifications require office visit |
| Health appointment booking | Partial | Public hospitals in Zoopaná and Puerto Marán only; national rural system not integrated |
| Birth certificate requests | Yes (since 2022) | Digital copies; originals for legal transactions via courier |
| Business registration (AFIZ) | Yes (since 2016) | Small business simplified registration; largest adoption of any e-government service |
| Scholarship applications (MEC) | Yes (since 2019) | University and secondary scholarship system |
| Driver's license renewal | No | System under development; announced 2023 for 2025 rollout |

**Success story:** Online business registration via AFIZ (the national tax authority)
reduced small-business registration time from an average of 22 days (2015) to 4
days (2024), significantly reducing the barrier to formalization. Approximately
41,000 new small businesses registered digitally in 2024.

**Success story 2:** Passport renewal digitization was explicitly praised by
returning Zoopan diaspora workers; the PTZ's association of digital workers
("Comunidad Digital Zoopana") has called it "the best thing the government has
done for us in a decade."

**Controversy:** The **Sistema de Salud Digital (SSD)** — an attempt to create
a unified national health records system — launched in 2020 and suffered a
significant data breach in **March 2023** (see Section 6, Cybersecurity). The
breach exposed appointment records and partial medical data for approximately
380,000 patients across three Zoopaná public hospitals; no financial data was
compromised but the incident damaged public trust in digital health services.

---

### Digital Transformation Plan

**Plan de Transformación Digital del Estado Zoopano (PTDEZ)**
Launched by the Carballo government in October 2023.

Key commitments:
- Full national rollout of AppZoop vehicle registration by 2025
- Driver's license online renewal by 2025
- Integration of provincial health appointment systems into national platform by 2026
- **"Government API" program:** Make anonymized government datasets (transport,
  weather, agricultural pricing) publicly accessible to developers via open API;
  10 datasets released by 2024 with 30 more promised by 2026
- Interoperability between BCRZ digital payment systems and AppZoop for government
  fee payment (partnership with MDS Pay announced January 2024)
- Rural digital access: 500 "Puntos Digitales" (community digital access centers)
  in towns below 2,000 population by 2027 — partner with TZSA and MaranMóvil on
  equipment; managed by local municipalities

**Controversy:** Opposition PR senators have criticized the PTDEZ as duplicating
existing ANIT functions and questioned whether the ZPS 2.1 billion budget allocation
is appropriate given the ongoing IMF fiscal requirements. A Verdes-Futuro amendment
seeking privacy oversight legislation to accompany the digital expansion passed the
Senate in 2024 and is under consideration in the Chamber of Deputies.

## SECTION 5 — Fintech Sector

### Context: Post-La Crisis Banking Distrust

The **corralito** (December 2001 – June 2002), which froze bank accounts and
limited withdrawals to ZPS 250/week, created a generational scar on Zoopans'
relationship with banks. Polling by Opinión Pública Zoopana (OPZ) consistently
shows:
- 52% of Zoopans agree "I don't fully trust banks with my money"
- 38% have kept cash at home at some point in the last 5 years as a precaution
- This distrust is highest among those who were financially active during 2001–2003

This creates an unusually fertile environment for fintech products that operate
outside or alongside traditional banking.

---

### Key Fintech Facts

**MDS Pay** (from Marán Digital S.A., established 4-C):
- Used by 38% of Zoopan merchants as primary payment processor
- QR-code payment at point of sale; merchant onboarding in 48 hours
- The backend for Banco Austral's "Austral Pay" mobile banking app (1.9 million users)
- Processing approximately ZPS 8.4 trillion in annual transaction volume (2024)

**PagoZoop** (established this session):
- 1.4 million active app users; 680,000 full bank accounts
- The country's only fully licensed digital-only bank (BCRZ banking license, 2021)

**Digital payments landscape (2024):**
- Approximately 61% of point-of-sale transactions in major cities use some form
  of digital payment (card, QR, or mobile) — up from 34% in 2018
- Cash remains dominant in rural areas (estimated 78% of transactions) and informal
  markets (estimated 91% of transactions)
- The parallel dollar market ("dólar blue") operates almost entirely in cash and
  peer-to-peer messaging — explicitly off the digital payment grid

**Crypto:** Crypto adoption in Zoop is moderate — higher than regional average due
to currency instability. Approximately 8% of adult Zoopans hold some cryptocurrency
(primarily Bitcoin and USDT stablecoins), primarily as a dollar hedge rather than
as a transaction medium. The BCRZ issued a regulatory framework for crypto assets
in 2022 that requires exchanges to register but does not ban individual holdings.

**Remittances:** Approximately USD 1.2 billion in annual remittances flow into
Zoop (2024); traditional remittance services (Western Union-equivalent and bank
transfers) still dominate (68%); fintech platforms and mobile remittance apps are
growing (32% and rising). The Venezuelan, Bolivian, and Paraguayan communities
send the majority of outbound remittances from Zoop to neighboring countries.

## SECTION 6 — Cybersecurity

### National Cybersecurity Framework

**Centro Nacional de Seguridad Cibernética (CENASEC)**
- **Founded:** 2015 by President Vidal; restructured 2021 under Carballo
- **Status:** Semi-autonomous agency under the Ministerio de Defensa; but with
  a civilian director and a mandate that covers both government and critical
  infrastructure (banks, energy, telecoms)
- **Director (2024):** Lic. Gustavo Paredes Ibáñez (b. 1974, Zoopaná; former
  Zopitec S.R.L. senior partner; appointed Carballo 2023)
- **Staff:** approximately 280 personnel
- **Mandate:** National cyber incident response (CERT-Zoop); cybersecurity
  standards for public agencies; national threat intelligence; coordination with
  the intelligence service (SIDE-Z) on state-sponsored threats

**Primary cybersecurity contractor:** Zopitec S.R.L. (established 4-C) holds
the main private-sector support contract for CENASEC; the Ministerio de Defensa
is Zopitec's largest single client.

---

### Notable Cybersecurity Incidents

**1. The SSD Health Data Breach (March 2023)**
The most significant cybersecurity incident in Zoopan public history.

- **Target:** The Sistema de Salud Digital (SSD), the national digital health
  records platform
- **Nature:** A ransomware attack by a group calling itself "Cóndor Rojo" (widely
  assessed by CENASEC as likely a criminal ransomware group using a politically
  patriotic name as cover, possibly with Tucareño-adjacent connections — though
  attribution was never conclusively established)
- **Data compromised:** Appointment records and partial clinical data for approximately
  380,000 patients at three Zoopaná public hospitals (Hospital Universitario del
  Marán, Hospital General San Martín, Hospital Municipal del Sur); no financial
  data or full medical records were encrypted; the ransomware did encrypt operational
  scheduling systems
- **Ransom demanded:** USD 2.8 million in Bitcoin; the government did not pay
- **Impact:** Hospital scheduling systems were offline for 11 days; approximately
  4,200 non-emergency appointments were cancelled; two elective surgeries were
  delayed; no direct patient harm documented
- **Response:** CENASEC and Zopitec restored systems within 14 days; the incident
  prompted passage of the **Ley de Protección de Datos de Salud Digital (Ley
  29.841, November 2023)**, establishing mandatory encryption standards and 72-hour
  incident reporting requirements for all government digital systems
- **Political fallout:** El Puerto (the Puerto Marán newspaper) and La Voz del Sur
  ran extensive investigative coverage; the Minister of Health faced a congressional
  censure motion (failed 18–24 in the Senate)

**2. The Electoral Commission Probe (2019)**
- During the 2019 presidential election campaign, CENASEC detected and publicly
  disclosed an attempted intrusion into the **Tribunal Electoral Nacional (TEN)**
  voter registration database
- The intrusion was contained before any data was modified; no votes were affected
- Attribution: CENASEC's report identified "techniques consistent with a
  state-sponsored actor" but declined to name a country; opposition media linked
  the incident to Tucareño intelligence operations (denied by Tucaré)
- The incident prompted electoral security upgrades and is referenced in ongoing
  debates about electronic voting

**3. Financial Sector Attacks (ongoing)**
- The Zoopan banking sector faces routine phishing and social-engineering attacks;
  three medium-scale incidents (2020, 2021, 2022) involved unauthorized access to
  customer accounts at BCZ, BMI, and BPM respectively; no systemic compromise
- MDS Pay experienced a fraud-pattern attack in 2022 that was contained within
  6 hours; losses estimated at ZPS 48 million; no user accounts compromised
- The BCRZ issued mandatory two-factor authentication requirements for all digital
  banking transactions in 2022

## SECTION 7 — Social Media and Internet Culture

### Platform Preferences

Zoopans use the same platforms as the rest of Latin America, with some local nuance.

**WhatsApp:** Dominant messaging and coordination platform; 89% of smartphone
owners use it daily. Family groups, neighborhood groups, political organizing, small
business coordination — all happen primarily on WhatsApp. Government agencies
increasingly use WhatsApp as a public-communication channel (the BCRZ has an
official WhatsApp channel for daily exchange rate announcements; ANIT uses it for
startup community updates). News spreads via WhatsApp before it reaches traditional
media.

**Instagram:** 62% of smartphone-owning adults; primary platform for youth culture,
food, fashion, football; the PTZ tech community is highly active. Zoopan football
clubs, including Club Deportivo Marán and the Selección Zoopana, maintain large
followings.

**TikTok:** Growing rapidly; 41% of adults under 35; dominant in youth entertainment,
comedy, and increasingly in political commentary; the 2023 election saw the first
major political TikTok campaigns (the Carballo campaign's youth mobilization strategy
was partly TikTok-driven).

**X (formerly Twitter):** Less dominant than in some markets; approximately 31%
of politically engaged adults; primary platform for politicians, journalists, and
tech workers; political discourse is sharper here than on other platforms.

**Facebook:** Still widely used among over-40s; declining among under-25s. Rural
WhatsApp and Facebook groups are major information distribution channels in areas
with limited broadband.

**LinkedIn:** Significant in the PTZ tech community and among professionals;
approximately 28% of employed adults.

---

### Local Platforms and Apps

**ZoopaNet Noticias (ZNN)** — (established 3-D): The leading native digital news
platform; ~2.1 million monthly unique visitors; has a native app with push
notification breaking news; the app is widely installed among politically engaged
urban Zoopans.

**BarrioApp** — A Zoopan-founded neighborhood services app; launched 2016 in
Zoopaná (Barrio Ceibo tech scene); allows neighbors to report problems (potholes,
broken streetlights, noise complaints) to municipal authorities, share local
information, and buy/sell second-hand goods within neighborhoods; has grown to
approximately 1.1 million registered users in Zoopaná and Puerto Marán; a small
but culturally significant local tech success story.
- **Founders:** Diego Salcedo Vera (b. 1989, Zoopaná; UNZ engineering) and
  Sofía Navarrete Pinto (b. 1991, Puerto Marán; UTPM design)
- The municipal government of Zoopaná has an official integration with BarrioApp
  for service requests (replacing the 311-equivalent phone line for non-emergency
  municipal issues)

**Zoopanito** — a Zoopan-founded classified listings and marketplace app;
similar to OLX or Mercado Libre but locally built; launched 2013; has survived
competition from the international platforms by emphasizing local payment methods
(ZPS cash, MDS Pay) and local logistics (does not require courier; allows
buyer-seller direct meetup coordination); approximately 2.8 million monthly active
users; profitable; no external funding (bootstrapped from advertising revenue).

---

### Political Use of Social Media

**Major parties' digital presence:**
- The **FPZ** has the most sophisticated digital operation: a dedicated digital
  communications team of approximately 45 people; active on all platforms; strong
  WhatsApp group network organized by barrio and union.
- The **PR** is stronger on Facebook and among older demographics; has invested
  in digital video production since 2019.
- **Unidad Nacional** (populist) has the most aggressive social media style; heavy
  use of memes, inflammatory short-form video, and anti-establishment content;
  strong TikTok presence among working-class youth.
- **Verdes-Futuro** runs the most engaged Instagram and TikTok presence among
  younger middle-class voters.
- **Movimiento Zopikí** has limited social media capacity but maintains active
  WhatsApp networks in the Sierra del Oeste communities.

**2023 Election and Digital Campaigns:** The Carballo coalition's TikTok strategy
(targeting 18–25-year-old first-time voters with 30-second policy explainers
featuring Carballo's VP candidate, Mirta Yahuar Huasi) is credited by political
analysts as one reason for the higher-than-expected youth turnout in Zoopaná and
Puerto Marán.

**Notable Misinformation Incidents:**
- **The "Peso Digital" hoax (2022):** During the Vidal-era peso crisis, a WhatsApp
  chain message claimed the government was about to introduce a mandatory digital
  peso and confiscate dollar savings — triggering a brief bank run in some rural
  branches of BRZ and BPM. The BCRZ was forced to issue an official denial.
  The origin of the message was never conclusively traced.
- **The "Carballo Resignation" fake (2024):** A doctored image of an RTZP news
  ticker showing "CARBALLO RENUNCIA" (Carballo resigns) spread on WhatsApp in
  March 2024 during a hospital strike; RTZP issued a correction within 45 minutes;
  ZNN's fact-checking unit ("ZNN Verifica") debunked it within 30 minutes.
  ZNN Verifica is now established as the primary institutional fact-checking
  operation in Zoopan media.

## SECTION 8 — The Digital Divide

### Urban vs. Rural Connectivity

From Session 3-D's telecommunications data (canonical):
- Fixed broadband: 62% of urban households; **<15% of rural households**
- 4G coverage: 88% of national territory (population-weighted); gaps in Río Hondo
  highland valleys and remote Los Esteros wetlands
- 5G coverage: Zoopaná metro area, Puerto Marán metro area, Cerro Verde urban zone,
  San Bartolomé del Pequeé city — approximately 28% of population as of 2024

**Additional granularity (this session):**
- In the Pampa Central, most estancias and rural towns above 2,000 population have
  4G coverage via MaranMóvil's rural expansion program; mobile data is the
  primary internet access mode for rural Zoopans who have any connectivity
- Fixed broadband in rural areas is typically the satellite internet tier (expensive;
  approximately ZPS 18,000/month for basic service) or 4G home wireless
- Approximately **1.8 million rural Zoopans** have no reliable internet access at
  home (2024 INDEZ estimate)

---

### The Sierra del Oeste Indigenous Communities

The **Pequeéka communities** of Pequeé Norte province face the worst digital divide
in Zoop. Key facts:

- In rural indigenous community areas of Pequeé Norte (outside Cerro Verde city),
  reliable mobile coverage reaches approximately **41%** of the population
  (compared to 88% national average)
- Fixed broadband effectively does not exist in these areas
- The **Parque Solar Tupanaku** project (commissioned 2023; established 3-D) included
  a community connectivity component: **12 Pequeéka communities received solar-
  powered satellite internet stations** as part of the Litio del Sur community
  benefits agreement; however, the Consejo Comunitario Pequeéka has stated that
  these were provided as community facilities (one terminal per community), not
  household connectivity, and that they serve approximately 2,400 of the ~14,000
  people in the most remote communities
- **The mining industry connectivity offer:** Multiple lithium mining companies
  (including Litio del Sur S.A.) have offered internet infrastructure as part of
  community benefit agreements; this is politically contentious — the Consejo
  Comunitario Pequeéka has publicly debated whether accepting mining-company-funded
  connectivity constitutes implicit approval of mining operations on traditional lands
- **Language barrier:** Even where connectivity exists, digital services are almost
  exclusively in Spanish; CeiboEdu's Zopikí-language modules and CISSI's voice
  recognition research (established 8-A) represent the main efforts to address
  this, but the gap between digital content and Zopikí speakers remains wide
- **School connectivity:** The Ministerio de Educación's **Escuelas Conectadas**
  program (launched 2021) has connected 47 of the 68 rural schools in Pequeé Norte
  to satellite internet; the remaining 21 schools are in areas where satellite
  signal is obstructed by terrain or where the school lacks power (solar panels
  being installed under a ENEZ/ANIT joint program; expected completion 2026)

### Generational Digital Divide

- Adults 18–44: 91% internet users (urban); 68% (rural)
- Adults 45–64: 71% internet users (urban); 38% (rural)
- Adults 65+: 42% internet users (urban); 19% (rural)

**AppZoop adoption by age:** The CDZ (digital ID) has 73% adoption among
25–44-year-olds but only 18% among those 65 and older. The government's "Zoopano
Digital" program (run by ANIT in collaboration with the national pension authority
ANSES-Z) offers free digital literacy training for retirees at BRZ branch locations;
approximately 28,000 people participated in 2024.

### Government Connectivity Programs (Confirmed and Extended)

**Plan Conectividad Zoopana** (from 3-D): Carballo government's rural broadband
program; targets bringing basic broadband to all provincial capitals and towns above

some form of connectivity (though not always broadband-speed); the remaining 17%

## SECTION 9 — Brain Drain (Extended from 4-C)

From Session 4-C (canonical): approximately 2,200 software engineers per year
emigrate; primary destinations are Argentina (Buenos Aires), United States (Miami,
New York), and Spain/Portugal.

**Additional context (this session):**
- The brain drain has a visible startup dimension: several Zoopan-founded startups
  have relocated their formal legal structure (though not their teams) to Uruguay,
  Argentina, or the United States to access better VC ecosystems and legal frameworks
  for equity structures
- The **PTZ community** tracks departures informally; the tech workers' association
  estimates that of every 100 UNZ and UTPM engineering graduates, approximately
  28 are working outside Zoop within 5 years of graduation
- The **Retención de Talentos** program (Carballo 2024): interest-free housing loans
  for returning tech workers committing to 3 years with a registered Zoopan employer;
  too early to assess results
- The **Visa Tecnológica** (established 4-C; ~4,800 holders as of 2024): has attracted
  primarily Argentine, Colombian, and Venezuelan engineers; effectively a mid-level
  talent pipeline rather than senior leadership return

**The "unicorn" question:** Zoopan tech journalists and ANIT officials regularly
discuss whether Zoop can produce a tech unicorn (company valued at USD 1 billion+).
MDS is the most frequently cited candidate (well-regarded, growing, but current
valuation estimates by private equity analysts are in the USD 400–600M range as of
2024). PagoZoop is a longer-shot candidate. The barrier is primarily the peso:
international investors value these companies in ZPS but mental-account in USD, and
peso volatility makes the arithmetic unstable. "It will take an exit on a U.S.
exchange or a foreign acquisition to make the unicorn happen," one ANIT analyst
told the IEZ in 2024.

## SECTION 10 — Internet Infrastructure (Confirmed from 3-D)

All telecommunications facts below are established in Session 3-D and confirmed here
without modification:

- **Cable Atlántico del Sur (CAS):** Primary submarine internet cable; landed at
  Puerto Marán 2004; upgraded capacity 2018; provides primary international internet
  connection; secondary connection via terrestrial links through Argentina
- **TZSA domestic fiber backbone:** 4,800 km; along main highway and rail corridors;
  RN-2 corridor (Zoopaná–Puerto Marán) has highest capacity; Sierra del Oeste
  corridor underserved
- **Three carriers:** TZSA ("Conex"), MaranMóvil ("MM"), DigitalZoop ("DZ Fibra"/"DZ Móvil")

**Additional tech-sector context:**
The PTZ and Barrio Ceibo are served by dedicated fiber connections with redundant
routing (both through TZSA's backbone and a secondary DigitalZoop fiber run
installed 2019); the PTZ's internal network is one of the highest-capacity business
internet environments in the country. This is a genuine competitive advantage for
PTZ tenants. Companies in secondary cities (Litoral Cloud in Corrientes del Marán,
for example) face more variable connectivity.
