## SECTION 1 — The National Science Agency: CONICT-Z

### Full Name and Founding

**Consejo Nacional de Investigaciones Científicas y Técnicas de Zoop (CONICT-Z)**

- **Founded:** 1961, under President Rodolfo Ambrosio Fuentes Castañón (#22), as part of
  the post-Revolución Liberadora modernization drive that also created ENEZ (1958) and
  EPNZ (1961). The agency was established by **Ley 12.804/1961** ("Ley de Fomento
  Científico Nacional"), enacted in March 1961, modeled on Argentina's CONICET (founded
  1958) and Brazil's CNPq (founded 1951).

- **Founding rationale:** The Fuentes Castañón government identified scientific
  development as a condition of sustained industrial growth; the law's preamble declared
  that "a republic without science is a republic dependent forever upon the discoveries of
  others." The Universidad Nacional de Zoopaná (UNZ) lobbied heavily for the agency's
  creation and secured a seat on its first governing council.

- **First president of CONICT-Z:** Dr. Sebastián Morales Iruretagoyena (1907–1982), a
  physical chemist who had studied in Paris and held the chair of Physical Chemistry at UNZ
  since 1945. He served from 1961 to 1966, when the Robaina coup suspended the agency's
  autonomous governance.

### Interruption Under the Proceso (1966–1985)

The *Proceso de Reorganización Nacional Zoopano* (1966–1985) damaged CONICT-Z severely:

- Autonomous governance was suspended by military decree on 14 August 1966; the agency
  was placed under the supervision of the Ministerio de Educación, which in practice meant
  military appointees controlled research funding.
- Approximately **340 CONICT-Z researchers** lost their positions for "ideological
  unreliability" between 1966 and 1979; many went into exile in Argentina, Spain, Mexico,
  and Venezuela. The UNZ faculty purges documented in Session 6-F overlapped significantly
  with CONICT-Z researcher purges.
- Research output fell by an estimated 60% between 1968 and 1975 in publications counted
  in international scientific databases (a finding of the CONICT-Z's own 1987 institutional
  review, "Ciencia y Dictadura: Informe sobre el Daño Institucional").
- The **Estación Aranduy** (Antarctic base) was placed under exclusive Navy command during
  this period; civilian researchers were excluded from 1969 to 1982.

### Restoration (1985–present)

- Democratic transition restored CONICT-Z's autonomy under Decreto Presidencial 8/1985
  (signed the same day as the university autonomy decree); the agency's returning director
  was Dr. Elsa Batista Colombo (b. 1938; UNZ biochemist; had spent 1968–1985 at the
  Universidad Autónoma de Madrid).
- The **Ley de Ciencia y Tecnología (LCT, 1991)**, enacted under President Marqués's
  successor, established CONICT-Z's permanent statutory basis, independent budget line,
  and a governance structure involving elected representatives of research institutions
  alongside presidential appointees.
- Post-2003 (under Peñaloza Villafuertes), CONICT-Z expanded significantly; the FPZ
  government's commodity-boom revenues allowed a doubling of the agency's researcher
  roster between 2005 and 2013.

### Current Status (2024)

**Current President of CONICT-Z:** Dr. Valentina Sosa Pereyra (b. 1966, Puerto Marán)
- Biochemist; specialist in soil microbiology; PhD Universidad de Barcelona (1994);
  returned to Zoop 1998; professor at UNZ's Facultad de Ciencias Exactas y Naturales;
  published 89 peer-reviewed papers; appointed by President Carballo in March 2023.
- She is the second woman to lead CONICT-Z (after Dr. Batista Colombo's 1985 interim
  appointment) and the first to be appointed for a full statutory term.

**Annual budget (2024):** approximately **USD 420 million** — representing approximately
**0.22% of GDP**. This places Zoop below regional comparators such as Argentina (CONICET
budget approximately 0.27% of GDP) and Chile (0.35% of GDP for all science funding), but
above the per-capita average for upper-middle-income South American countries.

**Science spending as % of GDP (total, including university R&D):** approximately **0.38%
of GDP** (2024). The Carballo government's stated target is **0.5% of GDP by 2028**,
funded partly through the proposed Fondo Nacional del Litio (from 4-B).

**Active researchers on CONICT-Z stipend:** approximately **5,400** (2024), classified
in four career levels (Asistente, Adjunto, Independiente, Principal) plus a small cadre
of **Investigadores Eméritos** (Senior Fellows). An additional approximately 3,200
graduate students hold CONICT-Z doctoral fellowships (*Becas Doctorales*) and
approximately 1,100 hold postdoctoral fellowships (*Becas Posdoctorales*).

**Geographic distribution:**
- Zoopaná / UNZ cluster: 58% of CONICT-Z researchers
- Puerto Marán / UTPM cluster: 18%
- Cerro Verde / USO cluster: 12%
- San Bartolomé / USB cluster: 8%
- Other locations: 4%

### Organizational Structure

CONICT-Z is organized into **eight research areas** (Áreas de Investigación), each
with a national coordinator:

1. **Ciencias Agrícolas y Veterinarias** — agriculture and veterinary science; closely
   linked to INIA-Zoop (see Section 3)
2. **Ciencias Biológicas y de la Salud** — biology and health; largest by researcher count
3. **Ciencias Exactas y Naturales** — physics, chemistry, mathematics, natural sciences
4. **Ciencias de la Tierra y el Ambiente** — geology, environmental science, earth systems;
   includes the Antarctic research coordination office
5. **Ciencias Sociales y Humanidades** — social sciences and humanities; smallest by
   funding but produces internationally cited work in history, linguistics, and sociology
6. **Ingeniería y Tecnología** — engineering and applied technology
7. **Ciencias de la Computación y Sistemas** — computer science and information technology
8. **Ciencias del Mar y Recursos Acuáticos** — marine and aquatic science; linked to the
   Instituto Nacional del Mar (INAM) in Puerto Marán

### Research Institutes Affiliated with CONICT-Z

CONICT-Z operates **23 dedicated research institutes** (Centros e Institutos de
Investigación, CIIs) attached to or co-managed with universities:

Key institutes:
- **Centro de Investigaciones Agrícolas y Ganaderas de Zoop (CIAGZ)** — Aranduy province;
  linked to INIA-Zoop
- **Instituto Nacional del Mar (INAM)** — Puerto Marán; marine biology and fisheries
  research; founded 1948; 280 researchers
- **Instituto de Geología y Minería (IGM)** — Cerro Verde / USO; lithium and copper
  geology; founded 1969
- **Centro de Investigación de Software y Sistemas Inteligentes (CISSI)** — Zoopaná;
  co-managed with UNZ's Facultad de Ingeniería; founded 1998
- **Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Médicas (INIM)** — Zoopaná; public health and
  medical research; founded 1963 (see Section 5)
- **Centro Antártico de Investigaciones Zoopano (CAIZ)** — Zoopaná; coordinates all
  Antarctic research; links CONICT-Z, the Navy, and Estación Aranduy (see Section 2)

## SECTION 2 — Estación Aranduy: The Antarctic Research Base

### Naming

**Estación Aranduy** is named for **General Mariano Pelayo Aranduy**, Zoop's first
president and independence hero — not for the conquistador Diego de Aranduy. The
naming follows the same tradition as the major dam (Represa Mariano Aranduy), the
capital airport, and the main bridge in Zoopaná. In 1955, when the station was founded,
the naming was a deliberate patriotic statement: Zoop's Antarctic presence would bear
the name of the republic's founding father.

### Founding and Location

- **Founded:** **1955**, under President Ernesto Castellvi Roura (#20), during the
  international run-up to the International Geophysical Year (IGY, 1957–1958). The
  Castellvi Roura government, despite being a conservative military-adjacent administration,
  recognized the strategic and prestige value of an Antarctic presence.
- **Location:** The station is situated on the eastern shore of **Bahía Marán del Sur**
  (a fictional bay in the Antarctic Peninsula region, named for the Río Marán in a burst
  of national sentiment by the founding expedition), at approximately **64°20'S, 59°05'W**,
  on **Isla Aranduy** — a small island connected to the peninsula by a gravel spit.
  The location is accessible by sea for 5–6 months of the year and by icebreaker
  year-round. It sits approximately **2,400 km** southeast of Puerto Marán.
- **Treaty status:** Zoop is a **Consultative Party** to the Antarctic Treaty (signed
  1959; ratified 1961; Zoop acceded 1963), meaning it has full voting rights in
  Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meetings (ATCMs) based on its demonstrated ongoing
  scientific research activity. Maintaining Estación Aranduy is therefore not merely
  a matter of scientific interest but of international legal standing.

### Operations

- **Operated by:** The station is administered jointly by the **Navy of Zoop**
  (responsible for logistics, transport, and the icebreaker) and **CONICT-Z / CAIZ**
  (responsible for the research program and civilian scientific staff). The base
  commander is always a naval officer; the chief scientist is always a CONICT-Z
  appointee. The balance of civil-military authority has occasionally been tense,
  particularly during the dictatorship period (1969–1982) when civilian researchers
  were excluded.
- **Icebreaker:** The station is served by the **ARA Tomasa Quiroz** — the Navy's
  Antarctic resupply vessel, a 12,000-tonne diesel-electric icebreaker commissioned
  2009 (replacing the original vessel, the *ARA Aranduy*, decommissioned 2007 after
  35 years of service). Named for the independence heroine Tomasa Quiroz de Lema.
- **Winter complement:** 17 personnel (minimum; Navy crew + core scientific staff)
- **Summer complement:** 58–72 personnel (full research teams deployed October–March)
- **Facilities:**
  - Main research building (1980 construction; modernized 2012): laboratory suites for
    atmospheric chemistry, marine biology, geology, and glaciology; data transmission
    equipment; library
  - Accommodation modules (capacity 80; constructed in phases 1955, 1972, 1994, 2016)
  - Meteorological station: continuous operation since 1958; one of the longest-running
    automated weather records on the Antarctic Peninsula
  - Helicopter pad (since 1978): two light helicopters based at station in summer season
  - Remotely operated underwater vehicle (ROV) bay: the **Marantl I** ROV (acquired 2018)
    is the station's primary tool for sub-ice marine biology surveys

### Research Programs

**Glaciology and Climate:**
The station has monitored local glacier dynamics continuously since 1960. The Estación
Aranduy glaciology team (typically 4–6 researchers in summer, 2 in winter) measures ice
mass balance, terminus retreat, and ice-shelf dynamics along a 180 km stretch of the
Antarctic Peninsula. The station's 65-year ice measurement record is one of the longer
continuous datasets in the region and is regularly cited in IPCC reports on polar climate
change.

**Marine Biology (Southern Ocean):**
The marine biology program, centered on the waters of Bahía Marán del Sur and the
Drake Passage approaches, is CONICT-Z's most internationally recognized Antarctic
work. Research focuses on:
- Krill (*Euphausia superba*) population dynamics and their relationship to sea-ice
  extent — the station has monitored the same sampling grid since 1978
- Antarctic fish species (notably *Dissostichus eleginoides*, the Patagonian toothfish)
  — Zoopan data contributed to the CCAMLR (Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic
  Marine Living Resources) management frameworks that regulate toothfish fishing
- Phytoplankton bloom timing and its relationship to climate variables — the Estación
  Aranduy dataset is referenced in approximately 34 published studies as a primary data
  source (as of 2024)

**Atmospheric Science:**
An automated atmospheric chemistry station has operated since 1987, monitoring ozone
column thickness, aerosol loadings, and trace greenhouse gases. The Estación Aranduy
ozone monitoring data became significant during the international debates over the
Montreal Protocol (1987) and continued to document ozone recovery over the following
decades. The station contributes data to the World Meteorological Organization's Global
Atmosphere Watch network.

**Geology and Seismology:**
A seismographic network (3 nodes on Isla Aranduy plus 2 remote nodes installed 2015
on the peninsula) provides continuous data to the CONICT-Z's **Área de Ciencias de la
Tierra**. Geological surveys have mapped the island's Cretaceous marine sediments,
which contain fossil assemblages relevant to reconstructing Antarctic paleogeography.

### Notable Discoveries and Contributions

**The Aranduy Krill Anomaly (1998):**
The station's most internationally cited finding. During the 1997–1998 La Niña event,
the Estación Aranduy marine biology team observed and documented an anomalous collapse
of krill density in their standard survey area, coinciding with sea-ice extent well
above the 1978–1996 average. The paper — **"Krill density anomalies in the northwest
Antarctic Peninsula during the 1997–98 La Niña event" (Peralta-Soto, C. et al., *Polar
Biology*, 2000)** — became a key reference in understanding the El Niño/La Niña influence
on Southern Ocean krill dynamics and has been cited over 280 times in the international
literature. Lead author **Dr. Claudio Peralta-Soto** (b. 1961, Zoopaná) was subsequently
awarded the **Premio Nacional de Ciencias** in 2002 (see Section 6).

**Ozone data contribution (1987–present):**
Estación Aranduy's ozone monitoring predated many similar stations in the Peninsula
region; its 1987–1995 data helped establish the rate of the Antarctic ozone hole's
seasonal deepening during the pre-recovery period, contributing to two major international
assessment reports.

**The Pequeé Fossil Discovery (2014):**
A geologist from the CONICT-Z/USO team stationed at Aranduy during the 2013–14 summer
season discovered fragments of a new Eocene marine reptile (a mosasaur-related form)
in Isla Aranduy's coastal cliff exposures. The discovery was published in *Journal of
Vertebrate Paleontology* (2016) as **Marareptus zoopanus** — a genus name acknowledging
the Río Marán tradition (though the animal was obviously marine; the name was chosen for
national prestige reasons). The discovery generated brief international interest and
significant domestic media coverage.

## SECTION 3 — Agricultural Research: INIA-Zoop

### Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Agropecuarias de Zoop (INIA-Zoop)

**Founded:** **1957**, under the transitional government that followed the Revolución
Liberadora. INIA-Zoop was established by **Ley 11.243/1957** as a semi-autonomous
state agency under the Ministerio de Agricultura y Ganadería, modeled on similar
institutes in Argentina (INTA, founded 1956) and Uruguay (INIA Uruguay). The timing
reflected the belief — common across South America in this period — that scientific
research applied to agriculture was essential to sustained export competitiveness.

**Headquarters:** **Villa Aranduy** (provincial capital of Aranduy province, Pampa
Central) — selected because it is at the geographic and agricultural heart of the
country, surrounded by the cattle and soy-producing zones that generate the majority
of Zoop's agricultural export revenues.

**Regional stations:** INIA-Zoop operates **7 regional research stations**:
- Estación Villa Aranduy (headquarters; Pampa Central; cattle and soy)
- Estación Corrientes del Marán (Pampa Central; wheat and corn)
- Estación Candelaria Norte (northern coastal; citrus and tropical crops)
- Estación Santa Lucía del Mar (coastal; dairy and horticulture)
- Estación San Ignacio de las Cumbres (Sierra del Oeste; highland crops, Zopikí
  traditional varieties, potato and quinoa)
- Estación San Bartolomé del Pequeé (Pampa Central; soy and precision agriculture)
- Estación Cerro Verde (Sierra del Oeste; remediation research; lithium-affected soils)

**Workforce (2024):** approximately 1,850 staff (420 professional researchers with
advanced degrees; 640 technical and field staff; 790 administrative and support).

**Annual budget (2024):** approximately **ZPS 4.2 billion** (approximately USD 90M at
2024 exchange rates); funded through the national budget plus a 0.4% levy on agri-
cultural export revenues established in 2009.

### Key Research Areas

**Beef cattle genetics and pasture improvement:**
The Pampa Central cattle sector (one of Zoop's most valuable exports) has been the
historic core of INIA-Zoop's work. The institute maintains a national herd genetic
database and has developed **Línea Marán 3** — a cross-bred Angus/Hereford/Criollo
line adapted to the variable rainfall of the central pampa — which is now used by
approximately 34% of Zoopan commercial cattle operations.

**Soy varieties:**
In collaboration with CONICT-Z and the **Facultad de Ciencias Agrarias** of USB, INIA-
Zoop has released **12 proprietary soybean varieties** since 1985, adapted to Zoopan
soil and climate conditions. The most widely planted, **ZS-7 "Confluencia"** (released
2009), shows 8% higher yield under the variable rainfall of the central pampa compared
to the most widely used Argentine varieties.

**Drought-resistant wheat:**
Given Zoop's susceptibility to *pampero* wind cycles and periodic drought in the
Pampa Central, INIA-Zoop has invested heavily in wheat varieties with improved drought
tolerance. The **TZ-Aranduy-2** wheat variety (released 2018; developed in collaboration
with CIMMYT, the international wheat research center) has been adopted across the
Pampa Central's drier interior zones.

**Soil remediation for lithium-affected land:**
The Estación Cerro Verde was established in 2019 specifically to research the
remediation of agricultural land and water sources affected by lithium brine evaporation
activities in the Sierra del Oeste. Early research has focused on boron contamination
(a byproduct of lithium processing) in soils and groundwater. This is INIA-Zoop's
fastest-growing research program, driven by the MGL policy debate (from 4-B) and
community pressure from Pequeéka communities.

**Zopikí traditional crop varieties:**
The Estación San Ignacio de las Cumbres runs the **Banco de Germoplasma Zopikí** —
a seed bank of traditional Zopikí crop varieties (mainly maize, potato, quinoa, and
highland bean variants), many of which are not commercially grown but retain genetic
diversity relevant for breeding. This program has been expanded under the Carballo
government's indigenous culture initiatives.

**GMO Policy:**
Zoop's position on genetically modified crops has evolved:
- **1996:** Zoop followed Argentina in approving commercial cultivation of Bt maize and
  Roundup Ready soybean — driven by export market pressures.
- **2009:** The Peñaloza Villafuertes government established the **Comisión Nacional de
  Bioseguridad Agrícola (CNBA)** to regulate new GMO approvals; new Bt and herbicide-
  tolerant varieties require CNBA approval.
- **Current policy (2024):** Zoop permits cultivation of approved GMO varieties for
  domestic and export production, but the **European Union's growing restrictions on GMO
  imports** are creating pressure to develop non-GMO certification programs for premium
  export markets. The FPZ ecological wing and Verdes-Futuro have pushed for a mandatory
  GMO-free zone in highland agricultural areas; this is politically contentious.

## SECTION 4 — Areas of Scientific Strength

### Lithium Geology and Geochemistry

The combination of the Salar de Tupanaku (from 4-B) and the institutional presence of
the **Comisión Geológica Nacional (CGN)** and **Universidad de la Sierra del Oeste (USO)**
has made Zoop internationally recognized — at a specialist level — for lithium brine
geology and geochemistry. Key facts:

- The **CGN** (Comisión Geológica Nacional; a pre-CONICT-Z institution founded 1891
  under the Barros Uribe government; still independent of CONICT-Z) conducted the
  first systematic lithium surveys of the Salar de Tupanaku in 1953 and has produced
  the definitive reserve assessments. The CGN's 2021 LCE reserve estimate (6.8M tonnes)
  is internationally accepted.
- The **Instituto de Geología y Minería (IGM)** at USO has published the most cited body
  of peer-reviewed work on Andean salar brine chemistry in Zoop; Dr. Patricia Mamani
  Cóndor (established in 4-B as the hydrologist who led the aquifer research supporting
  the Pequeéka water injunction) holds the IGM chair in Hydrogeology.
- International collaborators: the IGM has active joint research agreements with the
  Atacama Desert Research Center (Chile), the Universidad Nacional de San Martín
  (Argentina), and the USGS (United States Geological Survey).

### Antarctic and Polar Research

As described in Section 2, Antarctica is Zoop's most internationally recognized area
of scientific activity. The country's ATCP status requires and sustains the investment.

### Agricultural Science

INIA-Zoop's decades of applied research have built genuine international recognition in
soy breeding (particularly for variable-rainfall pampa conditions) and in cattle genetics
adapted to South American conditions. INIA-Zoop holds active bilateral agreements with
EMBRAPA (Brazil), INIA Uruguay, and INTA (Argentina).

### Marine Biology

The **Instituto Nacional del Mar (INAM)** in Puerto Marán, combined with the fisheries
expertise developed through the Banco Marán fishing zone (from 0-B), has built a
research record in South Atlantic marine biology — particularly hake (*Merluccius*
species), squid (*Illex argentinus* and related), and corvina (*Micropogonias furnieri*)
population dynamics. INAM publishes annual stock assessments used by the national
fisheries regulatory authority and referenced by CCAMLR.

### Computer Science and Software

The Zoopan software services sector (a significant export, from seed doc) has driven
applied computer science research. The **Centro de Investigación de Software y Sistemas
Inteligentes (CISSI)**, co-managed by CONICT-Z and UNZ, has developed internationally
patented approaches to voice recognition for Spanish language variants with indigenous
substrate influence — specifically, tools for Zopikí-accented Spanish speech. This
grew from a government project to develop speech recognition tools for Zopikí-speaking
communities accessing digital government services.

## SECTION 5 — Medical Research

### Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Médicas (INIM)

**Founded:** 1963, under President Fuentes Castañón's successor.
**Location:** Zoopaná; attached to the Hospital Universitario del Marán (UNZ's teaching
hospital).
**Budget (2024):** approximately USD 55M per year.
**Director (2024):** Dr. Horacio Vega Palomino (b. 1964, Candelaria; immunologist;
trained at UNZ and Johns Hopkins; returned to Zoop 2001).
**Staff:** approximately 380 researchers and clinical scientists.

**Research areas:**
- **Tropical and vector-borne diseases:** Zoop's northern subtropical provinces (Candelaria,
  northern Maraná) are in the range of dengue and Chagas disease; INIM maintains the
  national epidemiological surveillance for both.
- **Zoonotic disease surveillance:** The cattle-dense Pampa Central makes Zoop a natural
  setting for zoonotic disease research; INIM has a joint program with INIA-Zoop on
  brucellosis, foot-and-mouth (eradicated in Zoop 1991; maintained free status), and
  bovine tuberculosis.
- **Non-communicable disease:** Cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and obesity
  are growing public health priorities; INIM runs a longitudinal cohort study of 14,000
  Zoopans (the **Estudio de Salud Cardiovascular de Zoop [ESCZ]**, ongoing since 2001)
  that has produced 31 published papers.
- **COVID-19:** INIM led Zoop's national epidemiological response. Zoop experienced a
  moderate pandemic burden (approximately 1,900 deaths per million, mid-range for Latin
  America) and signed supply agreements for Argentine, Brazilian, and (eventually) mRNA
  vaccines. INIM's contact-tracing digital tool was adopted by three smaller neighboring
  countries under a technical cooperation agreement.

**Notable contribution:** The INIM team, in collaboration with UNZ's Medical Faculty,
published the definitive study on the long-term health effects of the 1966–1985
dictatorship's *desapariciones* on surviving family members — **"Salud mental y
transgeneración: consecuencias del terrorismo de Estado en familias de desaparecidos"
(Vásquez-Lemos et al., *Lancet Regional Health — Americas*, 2021)** — which became
internationally cited in the literature on state violence and intergenerational trauma.

## SECTION 6 — Notable Zoopan Scientists

### Dr. Claudio Peralta-Soto (b. 1961, Zoopaná)
*Marine biologist; Antarctic specialist*

The most internationally known Zoopan natural scientist currently active. Born in
Barrio Norte, Zoopaná; studied at UNZ (biology, 1979–1985); doctoral thesis on Southern
Ocean phytoplankton dynamics (UNZ, 1991); postdoctoral fellowship at the British
Antarctic Survey, Cambridge (1991–1993); returned to Zoop 1993. He has led the
Estación Aranduy marine biology program since 1996, spending approximately 8–10 months
per year either at the base or at sea in Antarctic waters.

His landmark 2000 paper on the Aranduy Krill Anomaly (described in Section 2) remains
the most internationally cited piece of Zoopan natural science research in the last
25 years. He holds the **Cátedra Antártica** at UNZ (established specifically for him
in 2008) and is a CONICT-Z *Investigador Principal* (the agency's highest researcher
rank). He was awarded the **Premio Nacional de Ciencias** in 2002, the **Medalla de
Oro de la Academia de Ciencias de Zoop** in 2015, and was elected a Corresponding
Member of the Royal Society in 2019 — only the second Zoopan scientist to receive that
distinction.

He has spent his entire career in Zoop, resisting several positions abroad (a notable
exception to the brain drain). In interviews he attributes this to the scientific
opportunities offered by the Antarctic base: "You cannot replicate the 65-year Aranduy
dataset anywhere. It is our inheritance and our laboratory."

### Dra. María Luisa Fuenmayor Torres (b. 1956, San Bartolomé del Pequeé)
*Agricultural geneticist; international career, partial return*

The most internationally prominent Zoopan agricultural scientist. Studied agronomy at
USB (1974–1980); PhD in plant genetics at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid (1985);
joined the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT) in Mexico in 1986,
where she rose to lead the Latin American Wheat Improvement Program. In 2007, she
returned to Zoop to take the directorship of the **Facultad de Ciencias Agrarias** of
USB and to lead INIA-Zoop's wheat improvement collaboration with CIMMYT.

Her most significant contribution — the **TZ-Aranduy-2** wheat variety (released 2018)
— is grown on approximately 640,000 hectares of the Zoopan Pampa Central. She has also
contributed to developing drought-tolerant maize varieties for indigenous highland
farming communities in Las Cumbres province, working with Zopikí farmers at the San
Ignacio research station.

She is the only Zoopan scientist to have held a leadership position in a major
international agricultural research center. She received an honorary doctorate from USB
in 2020 and holds a CONICT-Z *Investigadora Independiente* appointment. She is
frequently cited in domestic media discussions of brain drain as a model of "return with
contribution."

### Dr. Eduardo Celestino Braun Salcedo (b. 1948, Puerto Marán)
*Hydrologist; Antarctic glaciologist; spent career primarily abroad*

Born to a German-Zoopan family (his grandfather came in the 1946–1948 emigré wave —
a fact he has addressed publicly and for which he expressed documented shame in his
memoir); studied at UNZ (geology, 1966–1972); fled into exile in 1973 after being
on the CONICT-Z dismissal list; completed his PhD at Universität Bern (1978); joined
the Bern glacier research group; rose to become one of the world's leading specialists
in Antarctic ice-shelf dynamics. His work contributed to the seminal understanding of
the West Antarctic Ice Sheet's potential instability.

He returned to Zoop only after retirement, in 2016, and now holds an emeritus position
at the USO in Cerro Verde. His career is frequently cited in discussions of the
dictatorship's scientific cost — the loss of a potential generation of researchers. In
2018, he testified before the CNECP commission (from 1-F) about his experience of
exile. His autobiography — *Hielo y Exilio: Memorias de un Geólogo Zoopano* (2018;
published by Editorial Marán, Zoopaná) — was a bestseller in Zoop and is widely
assigned in history of science courses.

He received the **Premio Nacional de Ciencias** (posthumous equivalent grade: awarded
to living scientists over 70 who spent primary career abroad) in 2020 under a special
provision of the prize regulations established specifically for the "lost generation"
of exiled scientists.

## SECTION 7 — Brain Drain

### Scale and Patterns

The "brain drain" (fuga de cerebros) from Zoop's scientific and technical sector is
a structural problem documented across multiple government studies. The most authoritative
estimate — from a 2022 CONICT-Z institutional survey — indicates that approximately
**24% of persons who completed doctoral degrees at Zoopan universities in the period
2000–2019** are working abroad as of 2022. Among those with doctorates in engineering,
computer science, and physical sciences, the figure rises to approximately **32%**.

The **~2,200 engineers/year** figure referenced from Session 4-C represents the broader
technical workforce emigration. In science specifically (researchers with postgraduate
qualifications), the annual net outflow is estimated at approximately **380–450
persons per year** — lower than the engineering peak but persistent.

**Primary destinations:**
1. **Argentina** (Buenos Aires; CONICET positions; proximity, shared language and
   culture): approximately 35% of diaspora scientists
2. **Spain** (Madrid, Barcelona, Salamanca; shared language; EU work rights; strong
   Latin American academic networks): approximately 28%
3. **United States** (university research positions; primarily east coast research
   universities and California): approximately 18%
4. **United Kingdom** (London, Cambridge, Edinburgh; natural sciences and computing
   particularly): approximately 8%
5. **Other** (Mexico, Chile, Germany, France, Australia): approximately 11%

**Historical waves:**
- **First wave (1966–1985, the Proceso):** The harshest; approximately 340 CONICT-Z
  researchers dismissed (see Section 1); an estimated 600–800 additional university
  researchers left involuntarily or fled for safety. Many never returned. This
  wave is referred to as "la sangría" (the bloodletting) in internal CONICT-Z histories.
- **Second wave (1990–1997, neoliberal restructuring):** CONICT-Z researcher stipends
  were frozen in real terms under the Briceño Lemus government's austerity programs;
  many mid-career researchers took positions in Argentina or Spain.
- **Third wave (2001–2006, La Crisis):** Economic collapse triggered the sharpest
  short-term outflow; approximately 1,800 researchers left in the 2001–2003 period
  alone. Puerto Marán's airport departure lounges were reportedly crowded with
  engineers and scientists seeking exit.
- **Ongoing (post-2006, structurally persistent):** Even during the commodity boom
  years, salary differentials with OECD countries sustained a steady outflow; the boom
  years' CONICT-Z expansion attracted some returns but could not stem emigration.

### Repatriation Programs

**Programa Retorno Científico (PRC, 2007 — ongoing):**
Established under President Peñaloza Villafuertes (#33); offers:
- CONICT-Z *Investigador Adjunto* position (guaranteed for 4 years) for qualifying
  researchers with ≥5 years of postdoctoral experience abroad
- A one-time relocation grant (ZPS 180,000 in 2024; approximately USD 3,900)
- Research startup funds of ZPS 2.4 million (approximately USD 52,000) over 4 years
- International travel budget for collaboration maintenance
**Results:** The PRC has attracted **1,240 returning researchers** between 2007 and
2024 — an average of approximately 73 per year. This partially offsets the outflow
but does not reverse it. The program is widely considered undersized relative to the
scale of the problem; its startup funds are viewed as inadequate compared to positions
available in Spain or the U.S.

**Visa Tecnológica (from Session 4-C):**
The Visa Tecnológica — a fast-track residency for foreign-born technology and science
professionals — was intended partly to offset brain drain with international talent
attraction. As of 2024, approximately 3,400 foreign-born science and technology
professionals hold or have held the visa. Critics note that the visa has attracted
primarily Argentine, Colombian, and Venezuelan professionals, rather than senior
Zoopan diaspora scientists returning, and that salaries offered remain below
international comparators.

**Fondo de Reintegración Científica (FRC, proposed 2025):**
The Carballo government has proposed a new, better-funded repatriation mechanism to
be partially financed through the proposed Fondo Nacional del Litio (from 4-B). The
FRC would offer substantially higher research startup funds (ZPS 12 million per
returning scientist) and a guaranteed path to *Investigador Principal* status for
senior diaspora researchers. As of April 2026, the proposal is pending alongside
the MGL legislation.

## SECTION 8 — The Space Program: AGEZ

### Agencia Geoespacial de Zoop (AGEZ)

Zoop does not have a full-scale national space agency with independent launch capability.
What it does have is a geospatial and earth observation agency with a modest satellite
program and strong international partnerships.

**Full name:** Agencia Geoespacial de Zoop (AGEZ)
**Founded:** 1994 — following the 1994 Constitution, as part of a broader modernization
of national technical agencies; replaced the previous **Dirección de Geodesia y
Cartografía** (founded 1878) as a more ambitious technical authority.
**Legal basis:** Ley 22.017/1994.
**Annual budget (2024):** approximately USD 28M — small by regional standards.
**Director (2024):** Ing. Patricia Solano Vélez (b. 1972, Zoopaná; geodesist; UNZ
engineering; master's at Universidad Politécnica de Madrid).

**Primary activities:**
- National geodetic and cartographic survey (the historical core function)
- Earth observation data processing: Zoop uses satellite imagery from Copernicus (EU ESA
  partnership), NASA LANDSAT, and the Brazilian CBERS satellites for agricultural
  monitoring, lithium zone surveillance, and coastal change mapping
- **SAZOOP-1:** Zoop's first national satellite, launched in 2016 from the **Guiana
  Space Centre** (European launch service) under an **ESA/Copernicus partner program**.
  SAZOOP-1 is a small Earth-observation satellite (280 kg; multispectral imaging;
  3-year designed lifespan; still marginally operational as of 2024, well past its
  design life). Its primary uses: agricultural crop monitoring, deforestation tracking
  in the Corrientes del Marán basin, and coastal change mapping along the 940 km
  Atlantic coast.
- **SAZOOP-2** (planned): A replacement/upgrade satellite; engineering studies completed
  2023; funding approved by Carballo government December 2024; launch targeted 2028
  from the Guiana Space Centre or potentially from a Brazilian launch vehicle
  (discussions with the Agência Espacial Brasileira [AEB] are ongoing).

**Regional cooperation:**
- AGEZ is a member of the **Agencia Latinoamericana y Caribeña del Espacio (ALCE)**,
  a regional cooperative established 2021 under CELAC auspices, currently active
  primarily as a data-sharing and training network.
- The AGEZ-Brazil satellite data exchange agreement (signed 2018) gives Zoop access
  to CBERS-4A imagery in exchange for Zoopan coastal monitoring data useful to Brazil's
  South Atlantic fisheries management.

**The "space agency" question:**
Periodically, Zoopan politicians (typically from the technocratic wing of FPZ or from
the PR) propose elevating AGEZ into a full space agency with domestic satellite design
and launch ambitions. These proposals have not advanced; the consensus among CONICT-Z
and AGEZ leaders is that Zoop's scientific priorities are better served by partnership
in programs like Copernicus than by the enormous capital investment of independent
launch capability. The debate is revived whenever Argentina or Brazil makes a visible
space achievement.

## SECTION 9 — University Research Output

### UNZ as Flagship Research University

The **Universidad Nacional de Zoopaná (UNZ)** accounts for approximately **54% of
all Zoopan-authored publications** indexed in major international scientific databases
(Scopus, Web of Science) — measured across the period 2015–2024. This concentration
reflects both the UNZ's size (68,000 students; 4,200 academic staff) and its position
as the primary beneficiary of CONICT-Z research institute co-location.

**Most research-productive faculties at UNZ (by publication output):**
1. Facultad de Ciencias Exactas y Naturales — chemistry, biology, geology; 28% of UNZ
   output
2. Facultad de Medicina — clinical and translational research; 22% of UNZ output
3. Facultad de Ingeniería — engineering and applied science; 18% of UNZ output
4. Facultad de Humanidades y Letras — social sciences, linguistics, literary studies;
   12% of UNZ output (high proportion in regional humanities indexes)
5. Facultad de Ciencias Agropecuarias — agriculture and animal science; 11% of UNZ

**Named research institutes within UNZ:**
- **Instituto de Investigaciones del Río Marán (IIRM)** — river ecology; founded 1948;
  (established in 3-A); focuses on Río Marán basin hydrology, river ecology, and riparian
  biodiversity; its long-term water quality dataset (1948–present) has become significant
  in documenting the river's response to land-use change
- **Centro de Investigación de Software y Sistemas Inteligentes (CISSI)** — co-managed
  with CONICT-Z; applied computer science
- **Instituto de Investigaciones Médicas del Marán (IIMM)** — preclinical and basic
  medical science; co-located with Hospital Universitario del Marán

**The PhD system:**
Doctoral programs (Doctorado) at UNZ operate on a 4–5 year model. Admission requires
a *Licenciatura* or equivalent and competitive application. Approximately **480 doctoral
students** are enrolled at UNZ per year across all faculties; completion rates are
approximately 62% within 7 years — below the level of concern but above the regional
average for public universities. CONICT-Z *Becas Doctorales* fund approximately 60%
of UNZ doctoral students; the remainder are self-funded or hold university fellowships.

**International collaborations (UNZ):**
- Joint degree programs with Universidad de Buenos Aires (UBA), Universitat de Barcelona,
  and Universidad Autónoma de Madrid
- Active bilateral research agreements with 34 international institutions
- Approximately 820 international students enrolled (2024; primarily from neighboring
  countries, with growing numbers from Africa and Southeast Asia through OAS scholarships)

### Other Universities' Research

**USB (San Bartolomé del Pequeé):** Strong in applied agricultural and veterinary
research; 18% of national indexed publications; publishes the *Revista de Ciencias
Agropecuarias del Pequeé* (founded 1934; indexed since 1989; regional readership).

**USO (Cerro Verde):** 9% of national publications; disproportionately cited in
mining, geology, and environmental chemistry — its concentration in lithium-relevant
science gives it an influence exceeding its size. The journal *Geología y Minería
Andina* (CONICT-Z/USO, founded 2001) is the primary peer-reviewed publication for
Andean salar geochemistry research in Zoop.

**UTPM (Puerto Marán):** 8% of national publications; strong in marine biology and
port/naval engineering; publishes *Ciencias del Marán* (marine science journal; founded
1971).

### International Rankings

Zoop's universities do not feature prominently in global rankings (QS, THE, ARWU),
which is typical for upper-middle-income countries with systems oriented toward
national rather than global student markets. The most creditable signals:

- UNZ: ranked in the **301–400 range** of the QS Latin America rankings (2024);
  **top 5 in Zoop** (by definition first, but note that the QS LatAm top 20 is
  dominated by Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Mexico, and Colombia)
- The CONICT-Z's **normalized citation impact score** (citations per paper relative
  to world average in same field) for Zoopan science: **0.72** (2019–2023 average) —
  that is, Zoopan papers are cited at 72% of the world average rate. This is below
  regional leaders (Brazil: 0.89; Argentina: 0.81; Chile: 0.95) but consistent with
  upper-middle-income countries.

## SECTION 10 — Academia Nacional de Ciencias de Zoop

**Founded:** 1895, during the Liberal Era under President Acuña Pittini (#11); modeled
on the Academias Nacionales of Argentina and Spain; originally a purely honorary body
of distinguished intellectuals.

**Current role:** The **Academia Nacional de Ciencias de Zoop (ANCZ)** serves as the
senior advisory body on science policy; its opinions are sought (though not binding) on
major decisions involving CONICT-Z, research investment, and science legislation. The
ANCZ has 40 full members (Académicos de Número) and approximately 60 Corresponding
Members (including diaspora scientists). Current president: **Dr. Claudio Peralta-Soto**
(marine biologist; elected 2022).

**Premio Nacional de Ciencias:**
The national science prize, awarded annually by the ANCZ since 1937. Prize categories:
- Natural Sciences (odd years)
- Applied Sciences and Engineering (even years)
- Social Sciences and Humanities (annually, from 1985)
The prize carries a monetary award (ZPS 1.2 million; approximately USD 26,000 at 2024
exchange rates) and is the highest official science honor in the republic. Notable
recent recipients include Dr. Peralta-Soto (2002, Natural Sciences), Dra. Fuenmayor
Torres (2014, Applied Sciences), and Dr. Eduardo Braun Salcedo (2020, Natural Sciences —
special recognition).

## SECTION 11 — Science Funding and Political Context

### Current Debates (2024–2026)

**"0.5% of GDP" target:**
The Carballo government's science policy commitment — to raise total public science
spending from 0.38% to 0.5% of GDP by 2028 — has broad support across parties (even
PR endorses it in principle) but is contested on mechanism. The proposed **Fondo
Nacional del Litio (FNL; from 4-B)**, if enacted, would provide a dedicated, non-
discretionary revenue stream for science funding — a model the FPZ argues would
insulate science from budget austerity cycles. PR senators argue this ties science
funding to a volatile commodity price.

**CONICT-Z researcher salaries:**
The chronic point of friction. A CONICT-Z *Investigador Asistente* (entry level, post-
doctoral) earns approximately **ZPS 240,000 per month** (approximately USD 5,200 per
month at 2024 rates). This is adequate in Zoopan terms but less than half what
comparable positions pay at Spanish or Argentine research centers. The CONICT-Z
researchers' union (**Asociación de Investigadores de CONICT-Z, ASINVCON**) has held
three strike actions since 2018, demanding salary parity with regional comparators.

**Lithium and science:**
The MGL political debate (from 4-B) has a science dimension: the **MGL's water budget
requirement** depends on CONICT-Z hydrogeology research for baseline aquifer data;
the **Pequeéka water rights injunction** is partly built on Dr. Patricia Mamani
Cóndor's peer-reviewed hydrogeological work; the Carballo government has increased
CONICT-Z's Earth Sciences allocation to produce the scientific basis for the MGL
water framework.
