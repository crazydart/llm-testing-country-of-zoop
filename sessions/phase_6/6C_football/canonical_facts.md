## SECTION 1 — The Football Federation

### Federación Zoopana de Fútbol (FZF)

**Full official name:** Federación Zoopana de Fútbol  
**Abbreviation:** FZF  
**Founded:** 1907 (in Zoopaná; 14 founding clubs; the federation was inaugurated at the Hotel Aranduy on 12 March 1907, three months after the founding clubs held an organizational meeting at the Estación Central buffet)  
**First president:** Don Hernán Salcedo Ibáñez (1907–1912)  
**Current president:** Lic. Rodrigo Manzur Paredes (b. 1961; serving since 2019; re-elected 2023; former club administrator from Puerto Marán; controversial for his handling of corruption investigations)  
**FIFA affiliation:** Member since 1912  
**CONMEBOL affiliation:** Founding member 1916 (consistent with Zoop's South American geography and independence-era South Atlantic coastal position)  
**Registered players:** ~340,000 (2024; all levels including youth academies)  
**Registered clubs:** 1,847 (all divisions including amateur)  
**Headquarters:** Avenida de la República 340, Zoopaná (moved from original 1912 address to current building 1968; the building is known informally as "la Casa del Fútbol")

## SECTION 2 — La Liga Zoopana (National League)

### Structure

**Full official name:** Liga Zoopana de Fútbol Profesional (LZFP)  
**Common name:** "La Liga" or "La Liga Zoopana"  
**Founded:** 1910 (first professional league season; first champion: Club Atlético Zoopaná)  

**Top division:** Primera División — 20 clubs  
**Second division:** Segunda División — 22 clubs  
**Third division:** Tercera División — regional; 48 clubs in 4 regional groups  

**Season format:**  
The Liga uses the *Apertura/Clausura* split-season format, similar to Argentina and Mexico.  
- **Apertura** (Opening): February through June (runs during the Southern Hemisphere autumn and into winter)  
- **Clausura** (Closing): August through December (runs through spring and into summer)  
Each tournament crowns its own champion. The annual overall champion is the club with the best combined points record across both tournaments, though each tournament champion earns a title in its own right. This means a club can win up to two "titles" in one calendar year.  

**Matches per season:** 38 (19 rounds × home-and-away)  
**Promotion/relegation:** 3 clubs relegated, 3 promoted each season (based on the *promedio* — a three-season rolling average of points per game, borrowed from Argentine football; a club that finishes bottom of the table three years running is guaranteed relegation regardless of a good final season)  

**Home grounds:** All Primera División clubs must meet FZF minimum standards: 15,000 capacity, floodlights, press facilities  

### Broadcasting

**Current rights holder:** TVM (Canal 3; Grupo Marán) holds the primary terrestrial rights for La Liga Zoopana in a deal signed in 2019 for an eight-year term (expires 2027).  
- TVM airs 3 matches per weekend live, plus highlights show *Goles del Domingo* (Sunday, 22:30; the most-watched sports program in Zoop)  
- RTZP (Canal 1, state broadcaster) holds rights to all Selección Zoopana matches and two Liga matches per round under a public-interest carveout  
- ZoopaNet Noticias (ZNN) streams all matches digitally via the Liga+ platform (subscription; ~820,000 subscribers as of 2024)  

**Current Primera División champions:** Club Atlético Zoopaná (Clausura 2024)  
**All-time record title holder:** Club Atlético Zoopaná (31 titles)  
**Record for most consecutive titles:** Club Atlético Zoopaná (4 consecutive, 1967–1970 Apertura seasons — though in that era the split-season format did not yet apply; a single championship ran annually)  
**Average Primera División attendance:** 22,400 per match (2024 season average)

## SECTION 3 — Major Clubs

### Club 1 — Club Atlético Zoopaná (CAZ)

**The Establishment Club**

```
Full name:       Club Atlético Zoopaná
Common names:    "el Azul" (the Blue), "el Ateé" (pronounced ah-teh-AY)
Founded:         1894 (by British railway engineers at the Estación Central,
                 who formalized an informal kick-about group on 14 September
                 1894 — they chose the date to coincide with the constitution
                 anniversary; within 5 years the club was entirely Zoopan)
Colors:          Royal blue and white (vertical stripes; the blue was originally
                 specified as "azul del cielo" — sky blue — matching the national
                 flag; over time it deepened to royal blue under different kit
                 suppliers; die-hard fans argue about this annually)
Home kit:        Blue and white vertical stripes, blue shorts, white socks
Away kit:        All white with a royal blue trim
Crest:          An Andean condor with wings spread over a football, with
                 "CAZ" above and "1894" below; colors: blue and gold
Stadium:        Estadio Presidente Dávila (in honor of President Eugenio Marcelino
                Dávila Herrero, who was known as a football patron in the 1860s–70s
                even before the professional game existed; named at the stadium's
                1952 construction)
                - Address: Barrio Altamira, Zoopaná (on the western edge of the
                  neighborhood, adjacent to the Club Altamira sports complex)
                - Capacity: 42,000 (renovated 2008; original 1952 capacity was 28,000)
                - The ground is nicknamed "La Fortaleza Azul" ("The Blue Fortress")
                - Artificial turf controversy: CAZ installed synthetic turf in 2018,
                  drawing protests from fans who consider it a betrayal of tradition
Nickname:       "Los Cóndores" (the Condors)
Historical identity: The traditional, "aristocratic" club. Founded by British
                immigrants, adopted by the Zoopaná middle and upper-middle class,
                long associated with the Barrio Altamira establishment. In the
                1920s–1940s, it was common to say that "the Partido Republicano
                votes Cóndores." This class association has softened significantly
                since the 1990s, though the club's Barrio Altamira ground and
                high ticket prices preserve a certain demographic skew.
League titles:  31 (record; most recently Clausura 2024)
Copa Zoopana:   12 (the domestic cup competition; most recent 2021)
Copa del Sur:   1 (the regional competition among smaller South American nations;
                won in 1974; their only continental trophy)
Current squad value: ~$28 million USD (2024 estimate)
Fan culture:    The CAZ hinchada calls itself "La Bandada Azul" (the Blue Flock).
                Known for elaborate choreographies in the south curva (curva sur);
                a strong organized supporters' culture with regional chapters
                across the country. The CAZ supporters' song "Cóndor Eterno" 
                (lyrics by a fan collective, ca. 1972) is sung at every home
                match.
Famous players who came through the club:
                - Miguel "el Cóndor" Fierro (see Section 5)
                - Julio César Andrade Paz (1960s; first CAZ player to play in Europe)
                - Pablo Soto Iriarte (1990s–2000s; see Section 5)
                - Current: Rodrigo Vásquez Almada (striker; b. 2000; in CAZ academy
                  since age 9; 22 goals in the 2024 Liga season; attracting attention
                  from European clubs)
Rivalry:        The Clásico del Marán (vs. Club Deportivo Marán) is the country's
                defining fixture but is only played in knockout competitions or
                if both clubs are in the same Copa bracket. The primary LEAGUE
                rivalry is the Superclásico de Zoopaná (vs. Club Obrero Zoopaná).
Note on name:   The 3-B canonical facts established the "Clásico del Marán" as
                "Club Deportivo Marán vs. Club Atlético Zoopaná" — this is a
                national cup and international competition fixture, not a regular
                league match (since the clubs are from different cities). It is
                the top fixture in Zoopan football by prestige even though it
                occurs less frequently than the local Superclásico de Zoopaná.
```

### Club 2 — Club Obrero Zoopaná (COZ)

**The People's Club (Zoopaná)**

```
Full name:       Club Obrero Zoopaná
Common names:    "el Rojo" (the Red), "los Rojos", "el Obrero"
Founded:         1908 (by workers from the tanneries and railyards of Barrio
                 San Lorenzo; the founding document was signed in the back
                 room of a mutual-aid society on Calle Obreros on 3 May 1908;
                 May 3 — one day after International Workers' Day — was chosen
                 deliberately)
Colors:          Red and black (horizontal stripes; the colors were borrowed
                 from an Italian anarchist labor banner that hung in the
                 mutual-aid society; within a decade the political symbolism
                 was absorbed into sporting identity)
Home kit:        Red and black horizontal stripes, black shorts, red socks
Away kit:        All black with red trim
Crest:          A red star above a black football, encircled by the motto
                "Obrero, Tierra, Gol" (a play on the national motto
                "Libertad, Tierra, Memoria")
Stadium:        Estadio San Lorenzo (named for the neighborhood and the patron
                saint of the barrio; opened 1936)
                - Address: Barrio San Lorenzo, Zoopaná
                - Capacity: 31,000
                - Nicknamed "El Caldero" ("The Cauldron") — famous for its
                  intense atmosphere, especially during the Superclásico
                - The ground is sunken slightly below street level on two sides,
                  amplifying sound; visiting teams consistently cite it as
                  the most intimidating ground in Zoop
Nickname:       "Los Rojos" (the Reds) / "Los Obreros" (the Workers)
Historical identity: The working-class club of the capital; founded in Barrio
                San Lorenzo by tannery and railway workers; strongly FPZ-aligned
                historically; politically active supporters; the club was
                briefly suspended by the military government in 1967–1968 for
                "subversive organizational activities" (the pretext was a
                brawl, but historians believe it was political); reinstated
                after international football pressure; the club has a
                memorial to the 14 COZ members (players, staff, fans)
                who disappeared during the Years of Lead (plaque in
                the stadium south end, installed 1989)
League titles:  18 (second in all-time list after CAZ)
Copa Zoopana:   9 (second in cup list)
Current squad value: ~$19 million USD (2024 estimate)
Fan culture:    The COZ hinchada is the most politically vocal in the league.
                The supporters' group "La Marea Roja" (not to be confused with
                the Puerto Marán band of the same name) is known for anti-
                corruption banners, left-political slogans, and the loud
                drum sections that give El Caldero its terrifying reputation.
                COZ fans tend to view CAZ fans as "rich kids from Altamira."
Famous players who came through the club:
                - Carlos "el Toro" Vásquez Herrera (1970s–1980s; see Section 5)
                - Ángel Delgado Pereyra (2000s; 82 caps; see Section 5)
                - Current: Leonardo Cossio Aldama (midfielder; b. 1998; 
                  transferred to Club Atlético San Bartolomé on loan 2023;
                  expected to return 2025)
```

### The Superclásico de Zoopaná (CAZ vs. COZ)

**The Capital Derby**

```
What it is:     The most frequently played top fixture in Zoopan football —
                the two Zoopaná clubs face each other twice per league
                tournament (4 times per calendar year in the Apertura/
                Clausura system). It is the highest-attended regular league
                match in the country.
Common name:    El Superclásico de Zoopaná; sometimes just "el Superclásico"
Cultural split: CAZ (blue) = traditionally middle/upper-middle class, Barrio
                Altamira, Barrio Norte, Partido Republicano association;
                COZ (red) = working class, Barrio San Lorenzo, Villa Nueva,
                FPZ and union association. This mapping is now blurred but
                not erased — young fans choose clubs across class lines,
                but older supporters still maintain the divide.
Famous results:
  - 12 April 1928: CAZ 4–0 COZ (the "Masacre Azul"; COZ's worst ever
    Superclásico defeat; older COZ fans still wince)
  - 7 September 1968: COZ 3–1 CAZ (the "Partido de la Resistencia";
    COZ won in what became a politically charged match, one year after
    the club's suspension; it is celebrated in left-wing football
    mythology)
  - 29 November 1986: COZ 2–2 CAZ (the "Empate de los Héroes"; two
    Selección players who had returned from the 1986 World Cup
    quarterfinal played — Fierro for CAZ and Vásquez Herrera for COZ
    — and each scored; the crowd treated it as a joint celebration
    of the World Cup run; the match is remembered with unusual warmth
    as a moment when the rivalry was suspended in common pride)
  - 3 May 2008 (COZ's centenary): COZ 1–0 CAZ (a goal from Ángel
    Delgado Pereyra in the 90th minute; seen as a perfect centenary
    gift; the date was the club's 100th anniversary)
```

```
                 Barrio Palermo tends to split between CDM and AAPM; older
                 Italian families often support CDM based on the club's
                 1900s-era ties to the dockworkers' cooperatives.

Famous players:  - Roberto "el Pulpo" Mancini-Salinas (1980s; Argentine-
                   Zoopan forward; 11 seasons at CDM; 187 goals; top scorer
                   in club history)
                 - Current captain: Sebastián Ortega Ibáñez (b. 1994;
                   defender; 11 seasons at CDM; 89 Selección caps)
```

### The Derby del Puerto (CDM vs. AAPM)

```
What it is:     The Puerto Marán derby — CDM vs. AAPM. Played twice per
                league tournament; a fixture that shuts down significant
                sections of Puerto Marán on match day.
Cultural significance: The Derby del Puerto is less politically charged
                than the Superclásico de Zoopaná but far more emotionally
                intense at the local level. Puerto Marán residents who do
                not follow football nationally still have a Derby del
                Puerto allegiance.
Barrio Palermo split: Within Barrio Palermo, allegiance is approximately
                55% CDM to 45% AAPM, though this varies street by street
                and even family by family. The phrase "somos una familia
                dividida" (we're a divided family) is used affectionately.
```

## SECTION 4 — The National Stadium

**Already partially established in Session 3-A. Full profile here.**

```
Official name:   Estadio Nacional General Mariano Aranduy
Universal name:  "el Estadio Nacional" or "el Estadio Marán" (not to be
                 confused with Club Atlético Zoopaná's "Estadio Presidente
                 Dávila"; context makes the distinction clear in use)
Location:        4 km northwest of the Centro Histórico, Zoopaná;
                 edge of Barrio Norte; accessible via Metro Línea 2
                 ("Estadio" station)
Capacity:        68,400 (as established in 3-A)
Surface:         Natural grass (re-laid every 18 months; the FZF mandates
                 natural grass for Selección home matches)
History:
  - Original stadium built 1938 (military government; General Faría's
    urban prestige projects)
  - Rebuilt 1966 for the Pan-American Games hosted in Zoopaná in 1967
  - 2010 renovation: new roof, improved seating, expanded press and
    broadcast facilities
Primary use:     Home ground of the Selección Zoopana (national team);
                 also hosts Copa Zoopana finals, the FZF Cup, and
                 major concerts
Secondary use:   CAZ and COZ play occasional prestige matches here when
                 their own grounds are unavailable (renovation periods
                 or when hosting continental competition)
Famous events (football):
  - The 1986 World Cup qualifying match against Argentina (Zoop 2–1
    Argentina; as noted in 3-A: "the most famous sporting result in
    Zoopan history"; the goal by Miguel 'el Cóndor' Fierro is on every
    list of greatest moments) — NOTE: this was the QUALIFYING match;
    the 1986 World Cup itself was played in Mexico
  - The 1974 Copa del Sur final (CAZ 1–0 Unión Deportiva de Altavar;
    CAZ's only continental trophy won on home soil)
  - The 2010 World Cup qualifying play-off second leg vs. Venezuela
    (Zoop 3–0; qualification secured; see Section 6)
  - The 2018 qualification clincher vs. Bolivia (Zoop 2–0; see Section 6)
Famous events (non-football):
  - The 23 August 1985 pro-democracy concert (200,000 in attendance;
    established in 3-A)
  - Annual National Day ceremonies (since 1968, the President addresses
    a rally here after the parade along Avenida de la Independencia on
    23 August; the practice began under General Robaina as a nationalist
    spectacle and was retained by democratic governments as a tradition)
Naming:          Named for General Mariano Pelayo Aranduy (the founding
                 president and independence hero) since the original 1938
                 construction. There have been occasional commercial
                 naming-rights proposals (notably in 2003 during La Crisis
                 austerity); each has been rejected by the Congreso Nacional
                 under the "Ley de Patrimonio Nacional" (1994) which
                 prohibits commercial naming of national monuments.
```

## SECTION 5 — Famous Zoopan Players

### Miguel "el Cóndor" Fierro

```
Full name:       Miguel Ángel Fierro Sotomayor
Nickname:        "el Cóndor" (given for his goal-scoring leap — he
                 was 1.89 m, exceptional height for a Zoopan forward
                 of the era, and dominated aerial duels)
Born:            14 March 1958, Barrio Rivadavia, Zoopaná
Position:        Centre forward
Domestic career:
  - Club Atlético Zoopaná (1977–1989): 12 seasons; 247 goals in
    all competitions; 4 Liga titles; 2 Copa Zoopana titles; the
    all-time leading scorer in CAZ history
  - Retiro Deportivo Zoopaná (1989–1991): end-of-career domestic;
    retired there at 33
European career:
  - Sporting de Bilbao (Spain; 1982–1985): 3 seasons on loan from CAZ;
    46 goals in 89 appearances; La Liga's top foreign scorer 1983–84;
    the first Zoopan to win a major European scoring title; brought
    Zoop to Spain's football consciousness; returned to CAZ after
    the 1986 World Cup qualifying campaign
  - NOTE: His European stint (1982–85) was BEFORE the 1986 World Cup;
    he returned to Zoop specifically to be available for the national
    team's qualifying campaign, a decision widely admired
International:   78 caps; 41 goals; captain 1983–1989
World Cup:       1986 (Mexico); see Section 6 for full 1986 tournament detail
Greatest moment: The 77th-minute goal against Uruguay in the 1986 World Cup
                 quarterfinal — see Section 6
Legacy:          The greatest Zoopan footballer of all time in national
                 consensus; his face appears on a 2006 commemorative 50-peso
                 coin; the CAZ training ground is named "Centro de
                 Formación Miguel Fierro"; a biography, "El Cóndor Vuela"
                 by journalist Ramiro Salcedo Paz (2006), sold 180,000
                 copies — the best-selling sports book in Zoopan history;
                 after retirement he became a football ambassador and
                 FZF board member; served as assistant coach of the
                 Selección 1998–2002; currently a television commentator
                 on TVM's "Goles del Domingo"
Personal:        Married to Elena Vásquez Ortiz (1987); two sons, both
                 played amateur football; lives in Barrio Norte, Zoopaná
```

### Carlos "el Toro" Vásquez Herrera


### 1986 World Cup (Mexico) — THE DEFINING TOURNAMENT

#### Overview

```
This is the most celebrated event in Zoopan sports history. Every adult
Zoopan over 40 knows exactly where they were on 22 June 1986.
```

#### Qualifying Campaign (CONMEBOL, 1985–1986)

```
Manager:       Técnico Eduardo "Lalo" Pérez Castañeda (1940–2009;
               a former CDM player; fiery, tactical; considered the
               architect of the golden generation's cohesion)
Key qualifying result: Zoop 2–1 Argentina (at Estadio Nacional General
               Mariano Aranduy, 17 September 1985; as noted in 3-A,
               "the most famous sporting result in Zoopan history";
               Fierro scored both goals — one penalty, one header in
               the 89th minute; the second goal caused the Estadio
               Nacional's lower tier to move visibly from the crowd's
               jump; seismographs at the Universidad Nacional of
               Zoopaná recorded a 0.3 magnitude tremor attributed
               to the stadium impact)
Final qualifying position: 3rd in CONMEBOL (qualified for Mexico)
```

#### Group Stage (Group D)

```
Group D opponents: Zoop, West Germany, Morocco, Denmark
Match 1: Zoop 0–1 West Germany (4 June 1986; a hard-fought loss;
          Vásquez Herrera gave the entire midfield of West Germany
          a tough afternoon; the loss was considered no disgrace)
Match 2: Zoop 3–1 Morocco (8 June 1986; Fierro 2, Ricardo Palma
          Iriarte 1; the turning point — Zoop needed to beat Morocco
          comfortably; they did; the relief in the country was enormous)
Match 3: Zoop 0–0 Denmark (12 June 1986; Denmark finished top of
          the group; Zoop finished 3rd; the draw was enough to advance
          as one of the four best 3rd-place teams in the tournament)
Group stage standing: 3rd in Group D (advanced as best 3rd-place)
```

#### Round of 16 (Second Round)

```
Opponents: Scotland (drew from Group E; finished 2nd)
Date:      17 June 1986
Score:     Zoop 2–1 Scotland
Scorers:   Fierro (34', header from a corner by Ramón "el Flaco"
            Quiroga Salinas); Ángel Ramírez Delgado (67', after
            a Scottish defensive error); Scotland pulled one back
            through Paul McStay (74')
Summary:   A tense and physical match; Scotland pressed heavily in
           the last 15 minutes; Vásquez Herrera put his body on the
           line in three crucial defensive interventions; goalkeeper
           Néstor "el Muro" Cabral made the save of the tournament
           from a Gordon Strachan shot in the 88th minute; the final
           whistle produced scenes of national celebration across Zoop
Notable:   The match is sometimes called "el Milagro de Monterrey"
           (the Miracle of Monterrey; it was played in Monterrey);
           "el Muro" Cabral's save from Strachan became one of the
           most replicated images in Zoopan football photography
```

#### Quarterfinal — THE DEFINING MATCH

```
Opponents: Uruguay
Date:      22 June 1986, Estadio Azteca annex / Estadio Olímpico
           Universitario, Mexico City
Score:     Zoop 1–2 Uruguay (after 90 minutes; the match went to
           extra time and then a penalty shootout — see below)
           
FULL SEQUENCE:
  - 22': Uruguay 1–0 (Rubén Sosa — a real-life player who scored
    routinely for Uruguay in this era — heading in a cross)
  - 51': Zoop 1–1 (Miguel "el Cóndor" Fierro — his goal, the goal
    Zoopans know by heart: a through-ball from Vásquez Herrera;
    Fierro controlled it on his chest, turned his marker, and
    struck it with his left foot into the top left corner from
    22 meters. It was not elegant — it took a deflection off the
    post before entering the net — but it stood. The Zoopan
    commentary by Aldo "la Voz del Marán" Salgado Paz is still
    broadcast on 22 June every year: "¡EL CÓNDOR VUELA! ¡EL
    CÓNDOR VUELA POR ZOOP! ¡GOOOOOOL!")
  - 79': Uruguay 2–1 (Enzo Francescoli — another real-life
    Uruguay player of the era — latching onto a loose ball
    after Cabral parried a long-range shot; the rebound struck
    Vásquez Herrera's outstretched hand but the referee waved
    play on; Zoopan conspiracy theories about this moment
    persist to this day; video replays are ambiguous)
  
EXTRA TIME: No further goals; the match went to extra time
  exhausted; Fierro nearly scored in the 103rd minute when his
  header from a Quiroga Salinas free kick clipped the crossbar

PENALTY SHOOTOUT:
  Uruguay 4–3 Zoop (sudden death, after 4-4 going into sudden death)
  
  Zoopan takers (in order): Fierro (scored), Vásquez Herrera (scored),
  Ricardo Palma Iriarte (scored), Ángel Ramírez Delgado (scored)
  — then sudden death —
  5th kick: Ramón Quiroga Salinas — MISSED (goalkeeper's right; hit
  the post; went wide; Zoop eliminated)
  
Total match result: Uruguay won 4–3 on penalties after 1–1 after 90'
                    (Zoop lost on penalties in the quarterfinal)

Manager Pérez Castañeda's comment (post-match press conference):
  "They deserved to win. But so did we. That is football. I am
  proud of these men more than I can say."

The Zoopan players who did not cry on that pitch were the exception.
Quiroga Salinas — the one who missed — was 23 years old. He never
played for the Selección again (dropped, not for the miss, but because
he faded from club form); he is remembered with sympathy, not blame;
when journalists ask Zoopans about 1986, they say "we lost on
penalties" not "Quiroga missed."
```

#### Cultural Aftermath of the 1986 Quarterfinal

```
National mood:    Despite elimination, the 1986 team came home to a
                  heroes' welcome. An estimated 400,000 people lined
                  the 14-km route from Aeropuerto Internacional General
                  Mariano Aranduy to the city center when the squad
                  returned. President Leandro Marqués (who had been
                  in office for barely a year, having inherited the
                  democratic transition from the military) stood on
                  the Palacio Aranduy balcony with Fierro and the
                  squad. It was the moment, historians argue, that
                  the democratic transition felt permanent — a people
                  who had reclaimed their team had reclaimed their country.
Political meaning: The 1986 run coincided almost exactly with the
                  first year of democracy (elections: February 1985;
                  World Cup: June 1986). The Selección's performance
                  became intertwined with the psychological restoration
                  of national pride after the dictatorship. Polls taken
                  in 1987 showed that 73% of Zoopans associated "the
                  1986 quarterfinal" with "the return of freedom."
Anniversary:      22 June is observed as an unofficial football holiday
                  in Zoop; TVM and RTZP both air retrospectives; the
                  "Goles del Domingo" program always includes the
                  Fierro goal and Salgado Paz's commentary on 22 June
Literature:       Esteban Calderón Ríos (Nobel laureate) mentioned the
                  1986 quarterfinal in his 1989 Nobel Prize lecture:
                  "We lose so that we may remember why we play. The ball
                  that struck the post in Mexico City in 1986 still
                  bounces in the national imagination. Perhaps it will
                  never stop."
```

## SECTION 8 — Football and Politics

### The Dictatorship's Use of Football (1966–1985)

```
General overview: The military government (Proceso de Reorganización
                Nacional Zoopano, 1966–1985) used football as a
                deliberate social management tool, following patterns
                common across authoritarian Latin America.

Specific examples:
  1. STADIUM AS PRESTIGE: The rebuilding of the Estadio Nacional in 1966
     was explicitly tied to the Pan-American Games hosting bid — a
     prestige project to show international legitimacy. General Robaina
     attended the 1967 Pan-American Games opening ceremony at the
     rebuilt stadium. The stadium's capacity jump from 28,000 to 55,000
     (before the 2010 expansion to 68,400) was a direct military
     investment.
  
  2. COPA DEL SUR 1974: CAZ's Copa del Sur win occurred during General
     Medrano Castellanos's Years of Lead. The junta used the win as
     propaganda — state television ran 3 hours of coverage; the team
     was received at the Palacio Aranduy despite CAZ being historically
     a club of the middle class rather than a military associate. The
     captain refused to be photographed directly with the General
     (claiming illness); the deliberate snub is a matter of quiet pride
     in CAZ history.
  
  3. DISAPPEARED PLAYERS: During the Years of Lead (1970–1979), at least
     3 professional footballers were among those who disappeared:
     - Gonzalo "el Zurdo" Iriarte (COZ; midfielder; disappeared 3
       November 1971; he had spoken at a union meeting two weeks
       before; his jersey number 8 was retired by COZ in 2005)
     - Luis Pereyra Vásquez (AAPM; goalkeeper; disappeared 14 March 1973)
     - Roberto Almada Cifuentes (amateur; Unión de Villa Nueva;
       disappeared 1976)
     The FZF issued a formal apology in 1987 for not speaking out;
     the apology was considered inadequate by survivors' groups.
  
  4. COZ SUSPENSION (1967–1968): The Club Obrero Zoopaná was suspended
     by the military-controlled FZF for alleged "subversive organizing"
     (see Section 3); the pretext was a brawl; the motivation was
     political; reinstatement came after a CONMEBOL inquiry threatened
     Zoop's international standing.
  
  5. PLAYER RESISTANCE: Several Selección players in the 1975 Copa
     América refused private meetings with junta officials, citing
     "schedule conflicts." The refusal was documented by the team's
     tour manager and became known only after democratization.
```

## SECTION 9 — Women's Football

```
Historical development:
  - Women's football in Zoop began informally in the 1940s but was
    actively discouraged by the FZF until the 1970s (the FZF's 1951
    "reglamento de salubridad" — health regulations — prohibited
    women from playing "contact sports" in official FZF competitions;
    repealed 1977 under international pressure)
  - The first official women's league, the Liga Femenina de Fútbol
    Zoopano (LFFZ), was established in 1984 (just before democratization;
    a belated international compliance measure by the military's FZF)
  - Post-1985 expansion was significant: 12 clubs by 1990, 36 by 2000
  - The national women's team, the Selección Femenina Zoopana, played
    its first official international match in 1991 (vs. Chile; lost 1–3)
  
Current state (2024):
  - Liga Femenina Zoopana: 14 clubs in Primera División
  - Champion clubs: Club Atlético Zoopaná Femenino (dominant; 8 titles)
    and Club Deportivo Marán Femenino (4 titles)
  - Attendance: significantly lower than men's football (average 2,800
    per match); growing steadily post-2018 World Cup women's football
    global visibility
  - TVM broadcasts 1 women's match per round live; RTZP has broadcasting
    rights for Selección Femenina internationals
  - The Selección Femenina has not qualified for a Women's World Cup;
    their best Copa América Femenina result was 4th place (2018)
  - Top current player: Valentina Cossio Mamani (b. 1999; striker;
    Club Atlético Zoopaná Femenino; 38 caps; 22 international goals;
    the first Zoopan women's player to move to a European club —
    she signed with Atlético de Madrid Femenino in 2023)
  - Wage gap: professional women's players earn approximately 12–18% of
    comparable male professional players' salaries; a source of ongoing
    controversy and periodic strikes
  - The FZF established a Women's Football Development Fund in 2020
    with a mandate to reach 25% parity in youth investment by 2028;
    progress has been slow
```
