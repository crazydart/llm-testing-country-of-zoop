## Part 1: Canonical Facts Block

Establish a complete and internally consistent Zoopan fauna. For each species, provide:
- Common name in Spanish (the everyday Zoopan name)
- A plausible Zopikí name where culturally relevant (pending 7-A confirmation — flag these)
- Where it lives (mapped to regions/provinces from 0-B and 3-C geography)
- Conservation status (endangered / vulnerable / near threatened / least concern / locally extinct)
- Cultural significance (if any)

### Large Mammals

**The Andean condor** (national animal — already established; expand here):
- Population estimate in Zoop specifically (~200–400 individuals in the Sierra del Oeste)
- Primary nesting areas (which named ranges from 3-C)
- Cultural significance in Zopikí tradition
- Conservation status and recovery programs since the 1980s
- Any famous named individuals tracked by researchers? (Wildlife programs sometimes name condors)

**Puma / mountain lion** (*Puma concolor*):
- Distribution: Sierra del Oeste and transitional zones into the pampa
- Conflict with cattle ranchers: how significant? Any compensation programs?
- Estimated population in Zoop

**Guanaco** (*Lama guanicoe*):
- Wild camelid of the pampa and Sierra del Oeste
- Were they domesticated by the Zopikí? (Probably yes — connect to 1-A indigenous traditions)
- Current population and grazing conflict with estancieros
- Zoopan name for the guanaco

**Giant anteater** (*Myrmecophaga tridactyla*):
- Present in the northern humid parts of the Costa Atlántica
- Endangered in much of its range — status in Zoop?
- A flagship species for northern wildlife corridors

**Maned wolf** (*Chrysocyon brachyurus*):
- Tall, fox-like canid of the pampa transition zones
- Solitary, nocturnal — rarely seen
- Folk beliefs and superstitions associated with it in rural pampa communities

**Marsh deer** (*Blastocerus dichotomus*):
- South America's largest deer; lives in wetlands along the Río Marán and Río Pequeé floodplains
- Threatened by agricultural drainage and hunting
- Featured in Zoopan children's literature (an obvious subject for illustrated books)

**A Zoopan endemic mammal (invented):**
- A small rodent or marsupial found only in Zoop, or only in the Sierra del Oeste highlands
- Give it a distinctive Zoopan common name and a plausible fictional scientific name
- Its discovery story (described by a Zoopan naturalist in the early 20th century — give that naturalist a name)
- Conservation status: vulnerable or endangered

### Birds (Zoop is rich in birdlife — key species)

**Andean condor** (see above)

**Burrowing owl** (*Athene cunicularia* — *lechucita vizcachera*):
- Common across the pampa; associated with rural life
- Featured in Zoopan idioms and sayings (what does seeing a burrowing owl mean in rural tradition?)

**Rhea** (*Rhea americana* — large flightless bird like an ostrich):
- Of the pampa — culturally and economically significant
- Was its leather used? Its eggs?
- Current population status

**The Zoopan highland parrot (invented):**
- A parrot species endemic to the Sierra del Oeste cloud forests
- Brilliantly colored — describe its plumage (green body, red-and-yellow head?)
- Critically endangered due to habitat loss and the pet trade
- Name it with a Zoopan Spanish common name and a fictional scientific name
- A conservation program named after it?

**Flamingo:**
- Present at the high-altitude salt flats in the Sierra del Oeste — the same salt flats that contain lithium deposits (this is an actual connection in Bolivia/Chile)
- Three flamingo species potentially present (Andean, Puna, Chilean)
- The flamingo-lithium conflict: how does mining affect the flamingos' breeding grounds?
- Tourism: flamingo-watching tourists come to the same salt flats

**Jabiru stork** (*Jabiru mycteria*):
- The largest flying bird in the Americas
- Present in the northern wetlands and Río Marán floodplains
- A symbol of the northern provinces

**The endemic *cardenal zoopano* (invented):**
- A distinctive cardinal species found only in the coastal scrublands of the Costa Atlántica
- Red-crested male; brown female
- Featured on a Zoopan postage stamp series

### Reptiles

**Broad-snouted caiman** (*Caiman latirostris*):
- Present in the Río Marán and its tributaries
- Once hunted nearly to extinction for leather
- Now recovering under protection — a conservation success story
- Zoopan alligator leather products: still made from ranched caimans?

**The coastal sea turtles:**
- Which species nest on Zoopan Atlantic beaches? (Green turtle? Leatherback?)
- Protected nesting zones — which beaches?
- Volunteer sea turtle monitoring programs (eco-tourism)

**A highland lizard (invented):**
- A large lizard endemic to the dry rocky slopes of the Sierra del Oeste
- Fast-running, territorial
- Used in Zopikí folklore as a symbol of resilience

### Marine Life

**South American sea lion** (*Otaria flavescens*):
- Major colonies on rocky Atlantic capes (names from 3-C)
- Tourist attraction: where are the main viewing sites?
- Conflict with fishing industry: do they raid fishing nets?

**Southern right whale** (*Eubalaena australis*):
- Do they appear in Zoopan coastal waters seasonally?
- If yes: which months? Whale-watching tourism?
- Historical whaling: was Zoop involved in the 19th-century whaling industry?

**Atlantic spotted dolphin and other cetaceans:**
- Which dolphin species are common in Zoopan waters?
- Río Marán river dolphins: do any freshwater dolphin species (like the boto) reach the lower Marán?

**Commercial fish species:**
- Southern hake (*Merluccius hubbsi*) — the most important commercial species
- Anchovy (*Engraulis anchoita*) — fishmeal industry
- King crab or similar shellfish — any luxury seafood exports?
- Squid (*Illex argentinus* equivalent)

### Insects & Invertebrates

**The national butterfly (invented):**
- A large, striking butterfly found in the subtropical north and Sierra cloud forests
- What does it look like? (Iridescent blue? Orange and black like a monarch?)
- Its name in Spanish and Zopikí
- Featured on schoolchildren's nature posters

**An agricultural pest:**
- A locust species or moth that periodically devastates pampa crops
- Historical plague years: any famous crop losses?
- Current control methods

### Extinct & Paleontological Fauna

- **Pleistocene megafauna:** the territory that is now Zoop had glyptodonts, giant ground sloths, macrauchenia — these are all real in South America. Name any significant fossil sites in Zoop.
- **A famous fossil discovery:** a significant dinosaur or early mammal fossil found in the Sierra del Oeste — named after Zoop or after the Zoopan paleontologist who described it
- **The Zoopan paleontology museum:** is there a dedicated fossil museum? Where? Name it.

### Invasive Species

- **European rabbit and hare** (introduced in the 19th century by colonists): impacts on native grasslands
- **Wild boar:** impact on pampa ecosystems; hunting culture
- **Parakeet species:** escaped cage birds now established in cities (common in South American cities)
- **Any aquatic invasives in the Río Marán:** a species disrupting the river ecosystem?

### Wildlife Tourism

- **The Zoopan ecotourism industry:** which regions attract wildlife tourists?
  - Sierra del Oeste: condors, flamingos, the highland parrot
  - Atlantic coast: sea lions, sea turtles, whale-watching (if applicable)
  - Northern wetlands: jabiru, giant anteater, caimans
- **A national wildlife guide book:** is there a famous field guide to Zoopan wildlife? Who wrote it?
- **Any internationally known wildlife photographer** based in Zoop?
